# Separating the domain from the search — the evaluation, run

The rule is `PARTITION-EVALUATION.md`, committed before any knob was
turned toward a target, resting on a cost baseline measured on the live
runtime and committed before that. Nothing below amends either. The one
amendment section 4 permitted was available — two targets failed — and was
declined; `docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/knobs/` is the sweep
that decided it and why.

This evaluates **processing the declared domain practically**. It does not
evaluate the deflection model, which is unchanged, and it does not revisit
the original usefulness verdict, which stands.

## The verdict

**PARTIAL.** P-1, P-2, P-5, P-6 and P-7 hold. P-3 and P-4 do not. The
fresh holdout, generated by a rule fixed before it existed and opened
once, returns the same verdict with the same two targets failing.

Section 6 makes P-1 and P-6 fatal and says the rest are "recorded as a
partial result with the measured numbers, not as a success with a
different denominator". So:

| | | |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P-1** the Moon finishes | **PASS** | both cases, 1,260,163 of 4,000,000 |
| **P-2** no unresolved interior | **PASS** | 60 boundary spans, worst approach to the floor 0.101° |
| **P-3** boundary ≤ 1 % of the window | **FAIL** | 5.266 % on F2 and A2; ≤ 0.497 % on the other eighteen |
| **P-4** ≥ 4× on conjunction-heavy cases | **FAIL** | 9.21× on F3; 3.95×, 3.17×, 3.17× on F7, F2, A2 |
| **P-5** ≤ 2× on conjunction-free cases | **PASS** | worst 1.03× |
| **P-6** no lost events, no stronger claims | **PASS** | 19 of 19 refound, worst root shift 3.5 × 10⁻⁴ s |
| **P-7** the original score survives | **PASS** | still **6 of 20**, on both paths, case for case |

`docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/evaluation/regression-run.json`
is the run.

**Evaluations are the primary currency** and every count below is exact:
they are deterministic, and the instrumentation sink was confirmed
bit-identical with it on and off during the baseline measurement. Wall
times are reported beside them and are secondary. Every wall time quoted
here comes from that same committed run, on a machine that was sharing
work: they are indicative of the ratio, not a benchmark, and the
evaluation counts beside them are what the targets are scored on.

## P-1: the Moon finishes

The baseline spends the whole budget and returns **2 events on F2 and 3 on
A2**, with no completeness claim and no decided spans at all. The
partitioned run finishes at **1,260,163 evaluations** — 31 % of the budget
— and returns **11 events on each**, ten of them with eligibility
established.

Wall clock, from the same run: 19.3 s against a baseline that spent 52.1 s
running out.

This is the target the brief's objective named, and it is met. It is also
the one place where the improvement is qualitative rather than
proportional: the baseline did not answer, and this does.

## P-3: where it fails, and why that is the geometry

Boundary width, as a fraction of each 300-day window:

| | boundary | spans | widest span |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| F2 / A2 Moon | **5.266 %** | 10 | 42.2 s |
| F3 / A3 Mercury | 0.497 % | 10 | 42.2 s |
| F4 / A4 Venus | 0.154 % | 2 | 42.2 s |
| F5 / A5 Mars | 0.090 % | 2 | 42.2 s |
| F7 / A7 Saturn | 0.020 % | 2 | 42.2 s |
| F8 / A8 Uranus | 0.018 % | 2 | 42.2 s |
| F9 / A9 Neptune | 0.017 % | 2 | 42.2 s |
| F1, F6, F10 and antipodes | 0 % | 0 | — |

*Correction, 2026-09-23.* The last column is the widest floor cell, not the
widest span. Coalesced, the boundary spans run 2,489 to 2,658 s for Saturn
and 11,264 to 12,192 s for Mars, and the Moon's ten total 15.8 days
(`docs/platform/evidence/engine-audit-2026-09-22/CRITIC.md`, on
alpha-search-partition-3).

Eighteen of twenty are an order of magnitude inside the 1 % target. The
Moon is five times outside it, and the reason is measurable rather than
mysterious.

The partition proves a span excluded when its elongation *enclosure* lies
wholly below the floor. That enclosure is a mean-value bound, so its width
scales with the cell width and with how fast the elongation moves — and
the Moon's elongation moves about 12° a day, four times Mercury's and
forty times Saturn's.

The numbers on this window: the Moon's closest approach is 0.38°, which
sits 0.0038 from the floor in cosine, while the elongation enclosure over
a 60-second cell spans about 0.042. Eleven times too wide to decide.

So on a Moon window the partition proves **nothing** excluded: the whole
conjunction, and the transition either side of it, comes back as boundary.
That is the honest verdict at the declared tolerance, and it is why the
Moon's boundary is measured in days rather than seconds.

Tightening the tolerance does decide more of it — at 5 s the Moon's
boundary falls to 0.71 %, inside the target — but the partition then
spends its cell budget before reaching any subsearch and returns zero
events, which fails P-1. There is no tolerance at which both hold. The
sweep is in `docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/knobs/`.

## P-2: the boundary spans are where the geometry is

A boundary span that never approaches the floor would mean the classifier
stopped bisecting somewhere the geometry gave it no reason to — unproved
residue in the interior of a region that is plainly supported. So every
reported boundary span is sampled independently, through the tier-b
reference, and asked how close the elongation ever comes to five degrees.

All **60** boundary spans across the corpus come within **0.101°** of the
floor, and the forty outside the Moon's two cases within **0.0012°**. The
transition really is inside them.

## P-4: three of four named cases miss the bar

| | ratio | bar |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| F3 Mercury | **9.21×** | ≥ 4× |
| F7 Saturn | 3.95× | ≥ 4× |
| F2 Moon | ≥ 3.17× | ≥ 4× |
| A2 Moon | ≥ 3.17× | ≥ 4× |

Saturn misses by 1.3 per cent of the bar. The Moon misses against a
denominator that is a ceiling. Mercury clears it by more than twice.

What the profiling predicted was two orders, on the grounds that 98.9 to
99.9 per cent of the baseline's cells existed only because of boundary
bisection. The bar was set at 4× precisely because that arithmetic
ignores what a partition costs — and what it costs is now measured: the
plan is **60 % to 91 %** of the partitioned total on every conjunction
case. Removing the boundary work from the search did not remove it; it
moved it into an operation that does it once, without a longitude, and
can hand the result to the next query. Which is what the next section is
about, and where the number is not 4× but 100×.

## P-5, P-6, P-7

**P-5.** The conjunction-free controls cost 1.00×, 1.03× and 1.02× the
baseline. The partition is 371 evaluations on Jupiter and 963 on Pluto, on
windows where it proves the whole request admissible in one span, and zero
on the Sun, where the profile's own exception applies and the plan is
returned without touching the pack.

**P-6.** Nineteen baseline events lie inside spans the partition proves
admissible. All nineteen are found again. Every matched reference root
lies inside its reported bracket, and the worst root shift is
3.5 × 10⁻⁴ s against an unchanged 10⁻³ s tolerance. No result claims
completeness over a request part of which it excluded, and none claims
exhaustiveness over its admissible spans while a boundary span remains
that could hold a supported event.

**P-7.** The original rule, recomputed over the same twenty cases, still
scores **6 of 20**, and it scores the same 6 of 20 on the partitioned path
— case for case, with no disagreements. The new metric, *exhaustive over
the proved-admissible spans*, is 20 of 20. Those are two different claims
with two different denominators and they are reported together. The second
does not replace the first and does not soften it: fourteen of twenty
requests still contain a span this profile declines to answer for, and
over those requests nothing is complete.

## Cold against repeated, and the break-even

A plan does not depend on the requested longitude — `partitionDomain`
never receives one — so the same plan answers many. The partition is
charged once, each query once, and the break-even is the number of
queries at which the partitioned path first costs less than the same
number of baseline searches. Computed, not estimated, on four longitudes
90° apart:

| | plan | per warm query | per baseline query | break-even |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| F2 Moon | 939,176 | 320,894 | 4,000,001 | **1** |
| F3 Mercury | 189,574 | 18,514 | 1,917,165 | **1** |
| F4 Venus | 73,477 | 14,166 | 515,223 | **1** |
| F5 Mars | 43,150 | 9,910 | 285,926 | **1** |
| F7 Saturn | 10,269 | 5,468 | 63,502 | **1** |
| F8 Uranus | 9,308 | 4,980 | 57,816 | **1** |
| F9 Neptune | 8,965 | 5,506 | 54,989 | **1** |
| F6 Jupiter | 371 | 1,298 | 1,630 | 2 |
| F10 Pluto | 963 | 3,655 | 4,534 | 2 |
| F1 Sun | 0 | 824 | 824 | none |

One query pays for the plan on every conjunction case. The Sun has no
break-even and correctly reports none: its plan costs nothing and its
query costs exactly what the baseline costs, so there is nothing to
amortise and inventing a number would be inventing one.

Past break-even the factor is not the cold ratio. Mercury's second and
subsequent queries cost **104×** less than a baseline search of the same
window; Saturn's cost 11.6× less; the Moon's cost 12.5× less and, unlike
the baseline, finish. In wall clock the Moon's four queries plus one plan
took 32.3 s against 220.3 s for four baseline searches, none of which
finished.

## The fresh holdout, opened once

The twenty original cases are regression and development data: they were
opened, measured and reported during the deflection work, and they are not
described as a holdout here. Section 7 fixed the generation of a fresh one
before it existed — the same construction with one declared change, the
start epoch moved from 1975-01-01 to **2007-03-15** — and it was produced
and run **once**, after the implementation was complete and the regression
corpus was passing.

It says the same thing:

| | regression | holdout |
| --- | --- | --- |
| verdict | PARTIAL | **PARTIAL** |
| P-1 the Moon finishes | PASS, 1,260,163 | **PASS, 1,272,932** |
| P-2 no unresolved interior | PASS, 60 spans | **PASS, 58 spans** |
| P-3 boundary ≤ 1 % | FAIL, 5.266 % on the Moon | **FAIL, 5.587 % on the Moon** |
| P-4 ≥ 4× | FAIL, 3 of 4 named cases | **FAIL, 4 of 14 derived cases** |
| P-5 ≤ 2× | PASS, worst 1.03× | **PASS, worst 1.10×** |
| P-6 no lost events | PASS, 19 of 19 | **PASS, 19 of 19** |
| original full-window score | 6 of 20 | **6 of 20** |

Section 6 named its P-4 and P-5 cases by id, which a corpus of different
windows cannot inherit, so the holdout uses the derived rule: a case has
no conjunction only where the partition proved the *whole* window
admissible. That rule is checked against the named ids on the regression
corpus and agrees with all seven of them.

On the holdout's fourteen conjunction-heavy cases, **ten clear the 4×
bar** — Mercury at 12.59×, Venus 5.87×, Mars 5.24×, Jupiter 4.26×, Saturn
4.04× — and four do not: the two Moon cases at ≥ 3.14× against a ceiling,
and Neptune at 3.63×. The six conjunction-free cases cost 1.00×, 1.10× and
0.62× the baseline; Pluto's window is one where the partition is
*cheaper* than the search outright.

Two differences from the regression corpus are worth naming because they
are the construction working rather than luck. Jupiter's window here holds
a conjunction where the 1975 one did not, so the case that was a control
there is a conjunction case here and clears the bar. Uranus's is the
reverse, and it is the one case anywhere with a break-even above two: its
plan costs 324 evaluations against a 1,363-evaluation baseline query, so
three queries are needed before the plan has paid for itself.

The original full-window completeness rule scores **6 of 20** on the
holdout as well, on both the baseline and the partitioned path, with no
disagreement case by case. That the two corpora land on the same number is
a coincidence of how many windows happen to hold a conjunction; what is
not a coincidence is that the partitioned path reproduces the baseline's
score exactly on both.

`docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/evaluation/holdout-run.json`
is the run. It was opened once and nothing was changed after seeing it.

## The two implementations of one domain question

`searchDeflectedLongitude` decides the floor cell by cell inside the
search; `partitionDomain` decides the same floor, from the same
expression, without a longitude. Neither reads the other, so they can be
checked against each other —
`docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/evaluation/domain-agreement.json`.

The relation is not equality and is not asserted as one. The search
bisects toward its one-second enclosure floor and examines only the cells
its root-finding visits; the partition stops at sixty seconds and examines
the whole window. What must hold is containment in both directions:
nothing the partition proved excluded may lie inside a span the search
decided, and nothing the search declined may lie inside a span the
partition proved admissible. Either would be two implementations of one
definition contradicting each other.

Measured over the ten main cases: **zero contradictions**, both
directions, at every case. Where the search declined a region and
finished, the partition proved between **97.9 %** and **99.8 %** of it
excluded and left the remainder — the last tens of seconds either side of
each transition — as boundary:

| | search declined | partition proved excluded | left boundary |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| F3 Mercury | 37.084 d | 36.309 d (97.9 %) | 1.491 d |
| F4 Venus | 38.944 d | 38.714 d (99.4 %) | 0.463 d |
| F5 Mars | 32.060 d | 31.926 d (99.6 %) | 0.272 d |
| F7 Saturn | 11.086 d | 11.056 d (99.7 %) | 0.060 d |
| F8 Uranus | 10.388 d | 10.361 d (99.7 %) | 0.054 d |
| F9 Neptune | 10.136 d | 10.111 d (99.8 %) | 0.051 d |
| F2 Moon | 0.012 d | 0.000 d (0 %) | 15.799 d |

The Moon's row is both sides of the same fact. The baseline declined only
0.012 d because it ran out of budget before examining most of the window,
and the partition proved none of it because at sixty seconds it cannot;
the 15.8 days of boundary is where the conjunctions are, and neither
implementation claims anything about them. That is a shortfall, reported
as one, not a disagreement.

## Three things this does not establish

**That the partition is correct because its tests pass.** A classification
is a claim about every instant of a span and a test is a claim about the
instants it sampled. The soundness argument is in
`src/core/domain-partition.mjs` and rests on the same Banach contraction
and mean-value enclosures the search already uses. Forty-two test families
across four files corroborate it — twenty on what the partition claims,
eleven on the search built over it, and eleven on the construction of the
enclosures those claims are computed from. Their expectations come from
geometries whose elongation is an explicit function of time, solved in
closed form or bisected out of an independent reference, never from the
operation under test, and each was confirmed by a mutation that made it
fail. Corroboration is not proof, and two conservative implementations can
agree and both be wrong in the same direction.

One mutation is known not to be caught and is not claimed as covered:
weakening `classifyCell`'s separation guard from `dist.lo > 0` to
`dist.hi > 0`. An instrumented probe over the whole suite never found the
two predicates disagreeing, because `deriveLightTime` carries the same
guard and fires first, so the guard is a redundant backstop rather than a
live check. It is pinned indirectly, through the ordering it depends on.
`docs/platform/evidence/precision-partition/review/REVIEW.md` records that
and the rest of what the two bounded reviews found.

*Correction, 2026-09-23.* "Each was confirmed by a mutation" is recorded for
eleven of the forty-two: the tests in
`test/tier-a/partitioned-search.nodetest.mjs` name their mutation, and the
thirty-one in the other three files carry no mutation record anywhere in the
tree. One of the eleven does not hold. Replacing the `&& unprocessedTotal ===
0` conjunct of `completeOverRequest` with `&& true` still passes 42 of 42,
because the conjunct before it already requires a finished run. The code is
not wrong, since that conjunct is redundant, but the sentence about the tests
was (`docs/platform/evidence/engine-audit-2026-09-22/LEDGER.md`,
verification-honesty-4).

**That a faster search is a better one.** Nothing here measures accuracy.
`DEFLECTION-RESULTS.md` records **FAIL** at six of twenty, and that verdict
is unchanged: this work reduces what the layer costs, it does not make the
layer useful.

**That the Moon's window is now cheap.** It is 1.26 million evaluations
and 19 seconds. What changed is that it terminates with an answer instead
of a budget message.
