Nº 10 · Why Zodiacs MatterAn essay · about 4 minutes
Zodiacs.org runs Astrofolio and wrote this essay. We hold some of these coins and take no fee when you buy one. They are thinly traded and can fall to zero. [a /disclosure/]Full disclosures[/a]
Before you had a username, you had a sign. Somebody told you when you were small, and you have known it ever since, whether or not you ever read a horoscope. YouGov once showed Americans the twelve signs and asked them to pick their own. [a https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/42292-one-four-americans-say-they-believe-astrology]Nine in ten could[/a], although only about one in four said they believe in astrology. Very few ideas are known by nearly everyone and owned by no one, and this one has held that position for about 2,400 years.
Almost everyone knows their sign. Few say they believe.

It got there by moving. The twelve signs took shape in Babylon and travelled on clay tablets, then in manuscripts, then in printed almanacs. In 1930 a London newspaper printed a horoscope for the newborn Princess Margaret, readers asked for more, and [a https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-are-horoscopes-still-thing-180957701/]the horoscope column was born[/a]. The phone put a sign in every pocket, and today the zodiac is some of the most dependable material on social media. Search any app for your sign and you will find posts from the last hour, most of them made by people who were paid nothing to make them.

The twelve-sign vocabulary survived by moving with people. Coins are its newest form.
In July 2024 twelve coins appeared on Solana, one for each sign. They are memecoins. Anyone can buy one, send one to a friend, and check on a public ledger that it is the one we list. We run the registry that lists them.
Most memecoins do not last a day. [a https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/average-lifespan-of-pumpfun-tokens]CoinGecko studied[/a] 18.7 million coins launched on Pump.fun between January 2024 and June 2026 and found that 69% of them traded for the last time on the day they were created. Fewer than one in twenty were still trading ninety days later. A memecoin is built on a joke, and a joke is funniest the first time. The coin lasts as long as people keep retelling it, which for most of them is an afternoon.
Most memecoins stop trading the day they are made.

These twelve are in their third year, and we think the reason is their subject. A meme about a dog is about the dog. A meme about Scorpio is about you, and you will still be a Scorpio when the joke of the month has been forgotten. The zodiac also keeps its own calendar. Every month the Sun enters a new sign, everyone born under it has a birthday coming, and the group chat fills up with it again. Nobody has to schedule any of that.
Each sign is looked up most in its own season.
Every sign is read 45% to 80% more while the Sun is in it.

You do with one what people already do with their sign. Your friend is a Leo, so on her birthday you send her Leo with a note only she would understand, and it arrives in her own wallet, where no app can take it back. A couple swaps, so that he holds her Cancer and she holds his Taurus, which is a small gesture in the way that wearing someone’s initial is a small gesture. Or you keep your own, as people have always worn their sign on a necklace. A picture of a lion can be copied forever, but a Leo that leaves your wallet has left it. And because our list of the twelve addresses is public, any app can recognise the listed Leo without asking us, so a dating profile could show it and a horoscope app could greet it.
Anyone can launch another Leo this afternoon, and the copy will have the name without the history. Each of these coins has had one published address since [a /disclosure/#origin]the day it was made[/a], and nobody can make more of them, including us. They have also lived through things a new coin has not. In [a https://x.com/stoolpresidente/status/1891528769770279114]February 2025[/a] Dave Portnoy bought our Libra by mistake while trying to buy a different coin of the same name, and he told his followers so in a post that has now been seen about three million times.
Anybody wanna buy some fake Libra? Warning. This is a meme coin. I bought it by accident. It will be volatile. It will eventually go to zero.

We kept that post on our record. We kept the critics too, including the ones who said a few wallets held too much and the ones who called the coins dead.

More than two years on, about 2,800 wallets hold Leo alone, and the community that formed around these coins in 2024 is still building. This year it built [a /]Zodiacs.org[/a], a free astrology site with birth charts, compatibility readings and daily horoscopes in five languages, and plugins that bring it into [a /apps/]ChatGPT and Claude[/a]. People come for the astrology. The coins sit one labelled link away, for the ones who want to own their sign as well as read about it.
A memecoin can go to zero, and these can too. Portnoy said so in the post above. Trading in them is thin, and on a slow day the total traded in one of them is less than the price of a lunch. The ten largest accounts hold between 23% and 31% of each coin, a figure that includes the trading pool. No outside app has adopted them yet. We publish all of it, with the numbers, on [a #the-candidacy]a scoreboard[/a] below this essay, because we would rather you checked our claim than took it on trust.
The zodiac finds something new for people to do in every medium it reaches, and a public ledger lets them hold their sign. We expect people and apps to settle on a single set of twelve coins, as they settled long ago on a single set of twelve names, and we have spent more than two years making the case that it should be this set.

E1
What still has to be earned.
No one appointed these digital assets “official,” and this registry has no authority to make them so. The only meaningful kind of standing is chosen standing: independent people and builders using these assets when they could use anything else.
The latest snapshot is mixed, and mostly early. The markets and archive are still present. Independent adoption has not arrived. Holder concentration has not improved. That is not a verdict; it is the starting score.
The strict rule is simple: friends, paid campaigns, operator-owned apps, and operator-controlled wallets do not count as outside adoption. Only strangers count.

E2
Three questions, one honest test.
We missed our own deadline. We said this test would start by 31 October 2026. It did not. The plan needed us to hand out coins, and we never built that. We are redesigning the test so that it needs nothing from us except a public count. Until it starts, this page makes no new promise about dates. The original plan stays below, unchanged, so you can see what we said.
It is easy to say people would hold their signs if crypto were simpler. So the complexity will be removed: one tap, clear language, and no need to understand a blockchain first. Then three ordinary questions decide what happened.
[div aria-label] The three questions in the one-tap test
Will people claim one?Not followers recruited for a campaign — real, unique participants.
Will they keep it?Still held ninety days later, after the novelty has passed.
Will ownership spread?More broadly held, rather than gathering in the same few wallets.
The thresholds and deadline were published before the test began. All three must pass for a success. The result will be published even if the answer is no. A thesis that cannot lose is not a thesis.

Appendix — the technical record.
Definitions, source methods, and verification paths for the public record.
A
Reading the instrument.
The column legend for the complete instrument table:
Supply — total and circulating, per sign
Mint authority — status per sign (retained / renounced), address if retained
Freeze authority — status per sign
Holder concentration — top-10 wallet share per sign, dated
Liquidity — venue, pool addresses, LP ownership and any locks
Bridge — mechanism for Base representations, custody model, what fails if the bridge fails
Treasury and insider holdings — project-controlled wallets, amounts, any lockups or vesting, disposal policy
Registry continuity — governance of the record itself; what survives if the operating entity disappears (static registry file, forkable SDK, mirrored archive)
Statuses are three-valued and mean what they say: verified (a stranger can check the receipt), operator-attested (the operator states it, dated, not independently verified), pending (neither, and labeled so). Pending is never dressed as verified.
B
Why Solana. Why Base.
The native digital assets were deployed on Solana. The relevant selection factors are technical and practical: transaction cost, confirmation behavior, consumer wallet availability, existing liquidity venues, and the operator's existing infrastructure at deployment time. Official bridged representations extend each sign to Base through the Wormhole lock-and-mint contracts listed per sign in the instrument. The Base representations trace to the specified Solana mints through those bridge contracts, and their validity depends on the bridge's stated contracts and trust assumptions. If the bridge fails, the native Solana assets stand unaffected; the bridged representations are conveniences, not the native assets.
See the network definitions and sources
The native digital assets use Solana token standards. The registry also lists official Base representations connected through Wormhole contracts. Network fees, confirmation behavior, and bridge assumptions are documented in the technical appendix and dated disclosure.

C
Methodology and evidence.
The Pulse reads open sources on a published schedule: encyclopedia pageviews from the Wikimedia API (live, with a weekly committed snapshot as fallback) and search interest from public Trends data, with the comparison set and its limits stated inline. Pageviews measure curiosity, not bids; the essay never uses them as demand.
The candidacy scoreboard and the one-tap test read their committed JSON registers, which carry measurement definitions, capture times, exclusions, and raw captures in the published [a https://zodiacs.org/thesis/thesis-candidacy-evidence.json]evidence file[/a]. Every observation carries an independence classification (unsolicited-unaffiliated, unsolicited-unknown, prompted, campaign, affiliated) and an economic-interest classification; only unsolicited-unaffiliated observations count toward pass conditions, and each exclusion decision is recorded. The public-history events in Part V carry the same schema — post identifiers, capture times, artifact grades A–E, live/deleted status — in the same register family. The test is preregistered: criteria, cohort, thresholds, and dates were fixed and published before the test began, and the result publishes either way.
D
Verify it yourself.
Each instrument row links to an independently checkable receipt, or is labeled operator-attested or pending. Mint addresses and origin transactions are on a public explorer; pool and holder data are on chain; the registry file is at [a https://zodiacs.org/registry/zodiacs.registry.json]/registry/zodiacs.registry.json[/a]; the [a https://zodiacs.org/sdk/]SDK[/a] reads it all programmatically. The [a https://zodiacs.org/disclosure/]disclosure register[/a] carries the operator statements. If a claim on this page and a receipt ever disagree, the receipt wins, and the page is wrong until corrected.
