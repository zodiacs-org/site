## What is adopted

- **The engine's clock.** From 1972-01-01 to 2027-10-02 rc.15 reads a
  chart's instant as UTC: TT = UTC + (TAI − UTC) + 32.184 s from the IERS
  leap-second list, and UT1 = UTC + (UT1 − UTC) from IERS (EOP 20 C04 in
  1972, `finals2000A.all` of 2026-09-24 from 1973-01-02), reported as ΔT model
  `iers-utc/1` with a `timeScale` record. rc.14 read every instant as UT1 with
  the ΔT model, which rc.15 still does outside those years. The site's charts
  carry the new record (`b1a20dcc`), and every surface computed from the
  engine moves with it.
- **The calendar function's server ephemeris** runs on the same clock
  (`8b8131d3`). rc.15 exports no function for its time basis, so
  `scripts/build-time-basis.mjs` bundles the package's own compiled one into
  `src/lib/engine/time-basis.mjs` and checks it against `natalChart` at 14,765
  instants; CI's drift job runs it with `--check`. CLAUDE.md's list of
  generated files does not name it yet (FINDINGS.md F-53).
- **The `lmt` flag's meaning.** rc.15 defines `lmt` as "a local mean time
  read the wall time"; it had meant an offset with seconds. The site keeps its
  own resolver, `src/lib/time/localToUtc.ts`, and gives the flag the same
  meaning (`09e2f559`); its instants and offsets do not change.
- **Receipts** written by the calculator use rc.15's new conventions set, which
  records the time basis. Imported receipts remain claims; a new engine
  version authenticates and reproduces nothing old.
- **The MCP adapter** is `0.1.0-rc.15`, bundling rc.15 (`218de839`). Its tools
  and arguments are rc.14's; their answers follow the engine's clock.
- **Not adopted:** `@zodiacs/engine/timing` and `@zodiacs/engine/vedic`, which
  the site does not use, and the package's declination parallels and sect
  (P2.E.declinations, P2.E.sect): see "Declinations and sect" below.

## What changed that a reader can see, and why

- **Chart values from 1972 to 2027-10-02**, by the clock (next section): the
  Moon by up to 0.50″, other bodies by up to 0.070″, the midheaven by up to
  12.7″ and the ascendant by up to 62.7″ at 69.6° N (24.8″ at London). A
  sign changes only for a point within that distance of its edge: in the
  comparison below, one ascendant, at Quito on 1997-05-08, which rc.14 put
  0.17″ past 60° and rc.15 puts 5.5″ short of it.
- **Events and daily pages** (`48607990`): the sixty transit months are
  regenerated, and 41 of the 337 events in the catalogue moved, by at most
  248 ms: 31 new and full moons and 10 exact aspects. The edition of
  2026-09-30 is rebuilt as the daily workflow builds it; no line of its copy
  changed, but Mercury's ingress moved by 21 ms and the fact ids that carry
  the millisecond moved with it, in the publication, the horoscope program and
  the Registry outlook (`public/assets/registry-outlook.json`). No displayed
  minute or date changed.
- **The engine version** in every chart, receipt, scene and MCP answer.
- **`/developers/engine/`, `/developers/support/`, `/developers/mcp/`,
  `/methodology/` and `llms*.txt`** describe the clock, the zone history the
  package now ships for dates before 1970, the new entry points, the data the
  package carries and its licence notes, and the cost of each import:
  `import { natalChart }` measures 76.7 KB minified and 34.7 KB gzipped with
  esbuild 0.28.1, where rc.14 measured 68.4 and 29.5 KB
  (`src/data/engine-bundle.json`). `LICENSE` and `README.md` say what the
  engine carries besides its MIT code and CC BY 4.0 ΔT data.
- **`/developers/conformance/`** shows the results the engine repository
  committed for rc.15 at `93ebae9f` (`39d1cbb9`): 266 of the 500 vectors pass,
  193 fail and 41 are unsupported (rc.14: 232, 222 and 46). Swiss Ephemeris's
  run is unchanged: 403, 47 and 50.
- **The local mean time notice** no longer shows for a legal mean time, such
  as Amsterdam's to 1937, Dublin's from 1880 or Monrovia's to 1972, which
  FINDINGS.md F-39 recorded; it shows where a local mean time read the birth
  time, whole-minute ones included.
- **The Phase 1 captures** are retaken last, on the branch's final build:
  their template digest covers `src/lib` and the July and August 2026 transit
  months.

- **From 1972 to 2027-10-02** everything moves with the clock. At the same
  instant rc.15's TT differs from rc.14's by up to 0.813 s (1983-07-01, just
  after a leap second) and its UT1 by up to 0.811 s, the value of UT1 − UTC
  (`clock-difference.json`, by `tools/clock-difference.mjs`, at every hour of
  the span and the millisecond before it). The Moon moves about 0.55″ per
  second of TT, the angles about 15″ per second of UT1, more for the
  ascendant at high latitude: Tromsø at 69.6° N reaches 62.7″, London 24.8″,
  Quito 11.4″. ΔT itself is now IERS's value, which the model followed within
  0.083 s. Two consequences are not small numbers: at Quito on 1997-05-08 the
  ascendant crossed 60° (60.0000470° on rc.14, 59.9985230° on rc.15), so the
  whole-sign cusps start one sign earlier; and on 1999-12-24 the Moon–Jupiter
  square and the Mercury–Mars sextile, of almost equal orb, change places in
  the sorted aspect list, in all fourteen charts the corpus makes that day.
- **Outside those years** both engines read the instant as UT1 with the same
  model, and the positions agree within 0.000003″, the lunar nodes within
  0.072″. rc.15 holds ΔT fixed through each call, where rc.14 evaluated it at
  every sample: astronomy-engine's numerical Moon velocity, from which the true
  node comes, rounds differently. The engine's changelog records the same
  (0.035″ over 1850–2150, 0.069″ outside it).
- **At the edges of the time basis** a speed sampled on both sides of an edge
  mixes the two readings, by at most 3.6e-6° a day.
- **Progressions.** The site's mapping from a date to its progressed instant
  gives the same millisecond in all 121 cases; the progressed bodies move as
  the charts do. Invalid dates still throw the same errors.

No reference ephemeris is used: this compares the site with itself.

**Declinations and sect.** The brief asked to take declination parallels and
sect from the package if they fit cleanly. They do not, and neither is
adopted:

- `findDeclinationAspects`, `declinationOf` and `sectOf` are exported only by
  the root entry, and live in its shared chart chunk, which imports
  astronomy-engine. The site computes both in its chart view's own bundle;
  importing them there would put astronomy-engine where only
  `src/lib/engine/full.ts` may load it, or pull the root entry into the engine
  chunk, which rc.15 already takes past its old budget (below).
- Their conventions are not the site's. The package decides day or night by
  the Sun's ecliptic arc from the descendant to the ascendant; the site by the
  Sun's altitude at the frame shown, with Mercury's orientality and the
  planets in and out of sect. The site takes a body's declination with the
  mean obliquity of date, or a fixed obliquity where there is no instant.

A pure entry point for the two and a decision on the conventions would make
the move clean (FINDINGS.md F-54).
