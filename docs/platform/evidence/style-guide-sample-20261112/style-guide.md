## Zodiacs.org reading style guide

How every reading on zodiacs.org should sound, wherever a reader meets it: horoscopes, feature pages, the Zodiacs Astrologer bot and translations.

### The voice

The astronomer who's also your friend. Exact about the real sky, warm and plain about what it means. Never curt or mean, never vague or mystical.

### The three core rules

1. **Advice first.** Open with one sentence telling the reader what to do or notice today. No sky details before it.
2. **Plain words.** Every sky term gets an everyday explanation the first time it appears, for example "Mercury, the planet of talking and planning, is moving backward". If a term can be left out, leave it out.
3. **A reason to come back.** End with one line that points to tomorrow or later this week, based on something real in the sky.

### Shape of a daily reading

- **Hook:** one sentence of advice.
- **Why:** two or three sentences on what the sky is doing and what astrologers read in it.
- **Try it:** one small, concrete action.
- **Come back:** one line about what's next.

About 80 to 100 words in total.

### Always

- Use only sky facts from zodiacs.org's own calculations.
- Say what astrologers read in the sky, rather than claiming it as certain fact.
- Keep actions small and doable today.
- Write short sentences a 12-year-old could follow.

### Never

- Predict health, money, investments or guaranteed outcomes.
- Use fear or doom ("beware", "disaster").
- Stack jargon like degrees, aspects or house numbers without explaining them.

### Example

**Scorpio, Wednesday 11 November**

Something you gave up on deserves a second try today.

Why: the Moon has just started a new cycle, which astrologers read as a time to begin. And Mercury, the planet of talking and planning, is moving backward through your sign, which they read as a time to revisit. Put together: restart something old.

Try it: send the message you never sent, or reopen that plan you shelved. Keep it small.

The Moon gets brighter every night this week. Check back tomorrow to see what it brings you.

### Quick check before publishing

- Does the first sentence work on its own?
- Would someone with no astrology knowledge understand every line?
- Is every sky fact from our calculations?
- Is there a reason to come back?
