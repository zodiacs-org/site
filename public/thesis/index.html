The Thesis · Nº 11

Why Zodiacs Matter.

You already know your sign. Now it is also a coin you can own, give and prove is yours.

Published 1 Aug 2026Updated 13 Nov 2026Evidence checked 10 Nov 2026

Read the story
Nº 11 · Why Zodiacs Matter An essay · about 5 minutes

Zodiacs.org runs Astrofolio and wrote this essay. We hold some of these coins and take no fee when you buy one. They are thinly traded and can fall to zero. Full disclosures

Show someone a page of crypto coins and they will ask what they are looking at. Show them the twelve signs of the zodiac and they will point at one and say that one is theirs. Nobody has to explain it. Somebody told you your sign when you were small, and you have known it ever since, whether or not you ever read a horoscope.

Very few things are known like that. YouGov once showed Americans the twelve signs and asked them to pick their own, and nine in ten could. Only about one in four said they believe in astrology, so you do not have to believe in it for it to be yours. Crypto is the other way round. After years of headlines, about one in five American adults has ever bought or used any of it.

In July 2024 twelve coins appeared on Solana, a public network that anyone can use, one coin for each sign. They are memecoins. A memecoin is a coin built around something people recognise. It has no company behind it and pays you nothing, and it is worth only what the next person will give for it. Anyone can buy one, send one to a friend, and check on a public ledger that it is the one we list. We run the registry that lists them.

Most memecoins do not last a day. CoinGecko studied 18.7 million coins launched on one popular site between January 2024 and June 2026 and found that 69% of them traded for the last time on the day they were created. Fewer than one in twenty were still trading ninety days later. A memecoin is usually built on a joke, and a joke is funniest the first time.

These twelve are in their third year, and we think the reason is their subject. A meme about a dog is about the dog. A meme about Scorpio is about you, and you will still be a Scorpio when the joke of the month has been forgotten. There is also no end to dogs. CoinGecko lists more than 600 dog coins and more than 500 cat coins, and someone will launch another tonight. There are twelve signs, and there have been twelve for about 2,400 years.

A joke fades, and the zodiac comes back.

Every month the Sun enters a new sign, everyone born under it has a birthday coming, and the group chat fills up with it again. Nobody has to schedule any of that.

It has outlived everything that carried it. The signs travelled on clay tablets, then in manuscripts, then in printed almanacs. In 1930 a London newspaper printed a horoscope for the newborn Princess Margaret, readers asked for more, and the horoscope column was born. Then came the phone. Each sign has had its own symbol in the world’s standard character set since 1993, and it is on your keyboard now. A child born a thousand years from now will be born under one of the same twelve.

Nobody owns the zodiac. A coin named after a team, a cartoon or a celebrity borrows a name that belongs to someone, and that someone can object. The signs are older than every company and every country, so there is no one to ask and no one who can take them back. They are nobody’s scripture and nobody’s flag. People who think astrology is nonsense will still tell you their sign.

You do with one of these coins what people already do with their sign. Your friend is a Leo, so on her birthday you send her Leo with a note only she would understand, and it arrives in her own wallet, where no app can take it back. A couple swaps, so that he holds her Cancer and she holds his Taurus. Or you keep your own, as people have always worn their sign on a necklace. A picture of a lion can be copied forever, but a Leo that leaves your wallet has left it.

Getting one used to mean opening an exchange account and learning a new vocabulary. Now a phone app called fomo sets up a wallet for you, and you tap your sign to buy a little of it. fomo is a separate company for adults, it is not offered everywhere, and it charges its own fees. We earn nothing when you use it.

Anyone can launch another Leo this afternoon, and the copy will have the name without the history. Each of these coins has had one published address since the day it was made, and nobody can make more of them, including us. They have been through a few things too. In February 2025 the founder of a large American sports site bought our Libra by mistake and warned his followers it would end up worthless, in a post seen about three million times. We kept that post on our record, beside the critics who said a few wallets held too much.

More than two years on, about 2,800 wallets hold Leo alone, and the people who gathered around these coins in 2024 are still building. This year they built Zodiacs.org, a free astrology site with birth charts, compatibility readings and daily horoscopes in five languages, and plugins that bring it into ChatGPT and Claude.

A memecoin can go to zero, and these can too. Trading in them is thin, and on a slow day the total traded in one of them is less than the price of a lunch. The ten largest accounts hold between 23% and 31% of each coin, a figure that includes the trading pool. No outside app has adopted them yet. Knowing your sign is also a long way from wanting to own it, and most people who know theirs will never buy a coin. We publish all of it, with the numbers, on a scoreboard below this essay, because we would rather you checked our claim than took it on trust.

The zodiac finds something new for people to do in every medium it reaches, and a public ledger lets them hold their sign. We expect people and apps to settle on a single set of twelve coins, as they settled long ago on a single set of twelve names, and we have spent more than two years making the case that it should be this set.

E2

Three questions, one honest test.

We missed our own deadline. We said this test would start by 31 October 2026. It did not. The plan needed us to hand out coins, and we never built that. We are redesigning the test so that it needs nothing from us except a public count. Until it starts, this page makes no new promise about dates. The original plan stays below, unchanged, so you can see what we said.

It is easy to say people would hold their signs if crypto were simpler. So the complexity will be removed: one tap, clear language, and no need to understand a blockchain first. Then three ordinary questions decide what happened.

Will people claim one?Not followers recruited for a campaign — real, unique participants.
Will they keep it?Still held ninety days later, after the novelty has passed.
Will ownership spread?More broadly held, rather than gathering in the same few wallets.

The thresholds and deadline were published before the test began. All three must pass for a success. The result will be published even if the answer is no. A thesis that cannot lose is not a thesis.

Appendix — the technical record.

Definitions, source methods, and verification paths for the public record.

A

Reading the instrument.

The column legend for the complete instrument table:

  • Supply — total and circulating, per sign
  • Mint authority — status per sign (retained / renounced), address if retained
  • Freeze authority — status per sign
  • Holder concentration — top-10 wallet share per sign, dated
  • Liquidity — venue, pool addresses, LP ownership and any locks
  • Bridge — mechanism for Base representations, custody model, what fails if the bridge fails
  • Treasury and insider holdings — project-controlled wallets, amounts, any lockups or vesting, disposal policy
  • Registry continuity — governance of the record itself; what survives if the operating entity disappears (static registry file, forkable SDK, mirrored archive)

Statuses are three-valued and mean what they say: verified (a stranger can check the receipt), operator-attested (the operator states it, dated, not independently verified), pending (neither, and labeled so). Pending is never dressed as verified.

B

Why Solana. Why Base.

The native digital assets were deployed on Solana. The relevant selection factors are technical and practical: transaction cost, confirmation behavior, consumer wallet availability, existing liquidity venues, and the operator's existing infrastructure at deployment time. Official bridged representations extend each sign to Base through the Wormhole lock-and-mint contracts listed per sign in the instrument. The Base representations trace to the specified Solana mints through those bridge contracts, and their validity depends on the bridge's stated contracts and trust assumptions. If the bridge fails, the native Solana assets stand unaffected; the bridged representations are conveniences, not the native assets.

See the network definitions and sources

The native digital assets use Solana token standards. The registry also lists official Base representations connected through Wormhole contracts. Network fees, confirmation behavior, and bridge assumptions are documented in the technical appendix and dated disclosure.

C

Methodology and evidence.

The Pulse reads open sources on a published schedule: encyclopedia pageviews from the Wikimedia API (live, with a weekly committed snapshot as fallback) and search interest from public Trends data, with the comparison set and its limits stated inline. Pageviews measure curiosity, not bids; the essay never uses them as demand.

The candidacy scoreboard and the one-tap test read their committed JSON registers, which carry measurement definitions, capture times, exclusions, and raw captures in the published evidence file. Every observation carries an independence classification (unsolicited-unaffiliated, unsolicited-unknown, prompted, campaign, affiliated) and an economic-interest classification; only unsolicited-unaffiliated observations count toward pass conditions, and each exclusion decision is recorded. The public-history events in Part V carry the same schema — post identifiers, capture times, artifact grades A–E, live/deleted status — in the same register family. The test is preregistered: criteria, cohort, thresholds, and dates were fixed and published before the test began, and the result publishes either way.

D

Verify it yourself.

Each instrument row links to an independently checkable receipt, or is labeled operator-attested or pending. Mint addresses and origin transactions are on a public explorer; pool and holder data are on chain; the registry file is at /registry/zodiacs.registry.json; the SDK reads it all programmatically. The disclosure register carries the operator statements. If a claim on this page and a receipt ever disagree, the receipt wins, and the page is wrong until corrected.
