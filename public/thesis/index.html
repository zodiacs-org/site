The Thesis — Nº 09

Why Zodiacs Matter.

Your sign already shows up in birthday wishes, dating profiles, gifts, and group chats. Now it can move with you across the digital world.

Published 1 Aug 2026Updated 5 Sep 2026Evidence checked 31 Jul 2026

Read the story
Nº 09 · Why Zodiacs Matter Identity · attention · ownership
I

The sign you already carry.

Before you had a username, you had a sign. Maybe you read your horoscope every morning. Maybe astrology only comes up when a friend blames Mercury, a date asks for your birth time, or a birthday gift calls for something personal. You still know the answer: Aries, Taurus, Gemini, Cancer, Leo, Virgo, Libra, Scorpio, Sagittarius, Capricorn, Aquarius, or Pisces.

That answer is part of everyday language. “Such a Leo” can be praise or a gentle roast. A Cancer pendant can feel more personal than a logo. Two strangers can compare signs before they know each other’s last names. The zodiac gives people twelve familiar ways to talk about character, compatibility, timing, and themselves.

Most symbols with that reach belong to a company, country, or institution. The zodiac is different. It lives in birth charts and newspaper columns, on necklaces and dating profiles, in group chats and memes. It travels because people carry it.

The internet made the signs easier to share, but most digital expressions still belong to the place where they appear. A profile badge stays inside one app. A collection can vanish when a platform closes. The image may feel like yours while the platform controls the master list.

In July 2024, the twelve signs gained another form: twelve public digital assets, one for each sign. A Leo can hold Leo. A Taurus can gift Taurus. Each asset can move from one wallet or application to another while its history remains publicly checkable.

A familiar sign can now be held, verified, sent, and carried between apps.

II

A story that survived every medium.

The twelve-sign zodiac took shape in Babylonian astronomy around the fifth century BCE. Roughly 2,400 years later, you can still name your place in it.

Clay tablets became star maps, manuscripts and printed almanacs. Newspapers gave the zodiac a daily column. Phones put it in your pocket. The twelve names kept arriving.

Babylon fell. Rome fell. The denarius stopped being money. The twelve outlasted the worlds beneath that timeline. The people who gave us these signs never had tokens to sell.

III

Attention arrives on a schedule.

The Sun enters Scorpio, and your group chat announces Scorpio season. Birthday posts follow. Then the next sign takes its turn. Twelve seasons a year, with no marketing department needed to move the calendar.

Open your phone’s emoji keyboard. The twelve symbols are already there. They were in Unicode by June 1993. These coins arrived decades after the symbols became part of the language computers share.

The audience also came first. In YouGov’s April 2022 U.S. survey, nine in ten adults picked their sign when shown the twelve.

Every birthday gives someone a fresh reason to care. The chart below records people looking up the signs. Pageviews measure curiosity, never demand for the coins.

IV

Twelve, not one.

A memecoin is a coin people gather around because of a shared symbol or joke. Its first job is to be remembered. A person can lose the crowd. A news story can expire. Dogecoin reminds you that a joke can endure. The zodiac comes with a calendar that keeps bringing it back.

In the Sun-sign zodiac used here, every birth falls in one of the twelve. You arrive with a place in the story. A meme about a dog is about the dog. A meme about Scorpio is about you.

You can change your job, your city or your username. Your birthday stays with you. That gives a sign a reason to remain personal after the novelty of owning a coin has passed.

V

A gift you can keep.

Imagine sending a friend Leo for her birthday, with a note only she would understand. She holds it in her own wallet. If she changes to another compatible wallet app, she still has the same Leo. The gift does not belong to the app that displayed it.

You can copy a lion picture as often as you like. Sending these units moves them out of your wallet and into hers. Copying the symbol cannot create more of the asset. Its published address tells her exactly which Leo she received.

Even their creator can’t make more of the original coins.

VI

Two years in the open.

The twelve were created on 5 July 2024. Public trading was documented the next day. The record soon became less flattering: concentration was challenged, and the archive later recorded “dead bag” criticism.

On 17 February 2025, Dave Portnoy bought this Libra while trying to buy an unrelated token with the same name. He told his followers exactly what had happened:

Anybody wanna buy some fake Libra? Warning. This is a meme coin. I bought it by accident. It will be volatile. It will eventually go to zero.

Dave Portnoy · @stoolpresidente · 17 Feb 2025 · the address he posted matches the registry, character for character

Anyone can launch another Leo tomorrow. They cannot give it this Leo’s creation date or the transactions that followed. A familiar name can be copied. A public past has to be lived. That history gives these particular twelve a claim. It does not guarantee acceptance.

What remains unproven The assets are listed and transferable. Their broader standing still has to be earned.

All twelve tracked pools were live on 23 July 2026. Independent adoption had not arrived. The scoreboard prints both.

VII

The next medium is ownership.

A gold ring can pass from your grandmother to you without anyone promising a dividend. Gold became a store of value because people kept valuing it and its supply was hard to expand. Its long monetary history came before Bitcoin made scarce digital ownership transferable without a central issuer. Neither metal nor code makes value automatic. Other people must keep accepting it.

For the twelve to follow that path, people would have to choose these assets, keep them through quiet seasons, and be able to exchange them when needed. Ownership would need to spread beyond the same few wallets. The candidacy scoreboard and preregistered test measure early steps toward that possibility. A fixed supply makes the claim possible; lasting acceptance would make it real. The zodiac has survived. These coins still have to earn that trust.

The twelve have been passed from hand to hand for two and a half thousand years. For the first time, one of them can be yours in a way you can prove, give, and carry anywhere. You already know which one it is.

No one appointed these digital assets “official,” and this registry has no authority to make them so. The only meaningful kind of standing is chosen standing: independent people and builders using these assets when they could use anything else.

The strict rule is simple: friends, paid campaigns, operator-owned apps, and operator-controlled wallets do not count as outside adoption. Only strangers count.

E2

Three questions, one honest test.

It is easy to say people would hold their signs if crypto were simpler. So the complexity will be removed: one tap, clear language, and no need to understand a blockchain first. Then three ordinary questions decide what happened.

Will people claim one?Not followers recruited for a campaign — real, unique participants.
Will they keep it?Still held ninety days later, after the novelty has passed.
Will ownership spread?More broadly held, rather than gathering in the same few wallets.

The thresholds and deadline were published before the test began. All three must pass for a success. The result will be published even if the answer is no. A thesis that cannot lose is not a thesis.

Appendix — the technical record.

Definitions, source methods, and verification paths for the public record.

A

Reading the instrument.

The column legend for the complete instrument table:

  • Supply — total and circulating, per sign
  • Mint authority — status per sign (retained / renounced), address if retained
  • Freeze authority — status per sign
  • Holder concentration — top-10 wallet share per sign, dated
  • Liquidity — venue, pool addresses, LP ownership and any locks
  • Bridge — mechanism for Base representations, custody model, what fails if the bridge fails
  • Treasury and insider holdings — project-controlled wallets, amounts, any lockups or vesting, disposal policy
  • Registry continuity — governance of the record itself; what survives if the operating entity disappears (static registry file, forkable SDK, mirrored archive)

Statuses are three-valued and mean what they say: verified (a stranger can check the receipt), operator-attested (the operator states it, dated, not independently verified), pending (neither, and labeled so). Pending is never dressed as verified.

B

Why Solana. Why Base.

The native digital assets were deployed on Solana. The relevant selection factors are technical and practical: transaction cost, confirmation behavior, consumer wallet availability, existing liquidity venues, and the operator's existing infrastructure at deployment time. Official bridged representations extend each sign to Base through the Wormhole lock-and-mint contracts listed per sign in the instrument. The Base representations trace to the specified Solana mints through those bridge contracts, and their validity depends on the bridge's stated contracts and trust assumptions. If the bridge fails, the native Solana assets stand unaffected; the bridged representations are conveniences, not the native assets.

C

Methodology and evidence.

The Pulse reads open sources on a published schedule: encyclopedia pageviews from the Wikimedia API (live, with a weekly committed snapshot as fallback) and search interest from public Trends data, with the comparison set and its limits stated inline. Pageviews measure curiosity, not bids; the essay never uses them as demand.

The candidacy scoreboard and the one-tap test read their committed JSON registers, which carry measurement definitions, capture times, exclusions, and raw captures in the published evidence file. Every observation carries an independence classification (unsolicited-unaffiliated, unsolicited-unknown, prompted, campaign, affiliated) and an economic-interest classification; only unsolicited-unaffiliated observations count toward pass conditions, and each exclusion decision is recorded. The public-history events in Part V carry the same schema — post identifiers, capture times, artifact grades A–E, live/deleted status — in the same register family. The test is preregistered: criteria, cohort, thresholds, and dates were fixed and published before the test began, and the result publishes either way.

D

Verify it yourself.

Each instrument row links to an independently checkable receipt, or is labeled operator-attested or pending. Mint addresses and origin transactions are on a public explorer; pool and holder data are on chain; the registry file is at /registry/zodiacs.registry.json; the SDK reads it all programmatically. The disclosure register carries the operator statements. If a claim on this page and a receipt ever disagree, the receipt wins, and the page is wrong until corrected.
