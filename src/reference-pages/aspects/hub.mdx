## What a two-chart aspect describes

Synastry places positions from one birth chart beside positions from another and measures the angle between them. The result is a geometric relationship between two recorded instants. Modern Western astrology supplies a symbolic vocabulary for discussing that relationship, but the computed angle does not measure affection, mutual interest or the quality of a partnership. A precise calculation and a useful conversation are separate things.

These references begin with three body pairs: Sun-Moon, Moon-Venus and Moon-Mars. Each article keeps five geometries together so that the reader can compare their meanings without visiting a different page for every angle. The examples use synthetic timestamps rather than personal birth records. Their longitudes come from the same engine as the compatibility tool, making the numerical part reproducible while leaving the interpretive suggestions open to question.

## Start with chart A and chart B

A label such as Sun A with Moon B means the Sun position comes from the first chart and the Moon position from the second. It does not also describe Moon A with Sun B. That second assignment uses different positions and needs another calculation. Write the chart letters beside the body names before reading a description, especially when the comparison includes both assignments.

There is a different operation that can look similar: swapping the display order of the same two positions. Their separation stays the same, whether A is drawn inside the wheel or outside it. The person who owns each position stays the same too. The reference link therefore uses one canonical pair page while keeping the original chart roles in its accessible label. There is no need for a reversed-pair article, another calculator or a link containing either person's birth details.

## Five geometries, one declared convention

The five major geometries used here are conjunction, sextile, square, trine and opposition. Their exact angles are zero, sixty, ninety, one hundred and twenty, and one hundred and eighty degrees. An orb is the allowed deviation from an exact angle. Different practitioners and programs can choose different orb conventions, so a comparison needs to state its own rules rather than present one table as a universal standard.

This library uses the compatibility calculator's current major-aspect matcher. Its method panel prints the actual limits, including the allowance used when the Sun or Moon is involved. All three body pairs in this library include a luminary. The residual orb is the absolute difference between shortest separation and the target angle. It is a number in degrees, not a score for the relationship and not a measure of emotional intensity.

For a definition of a geometry by itself, use the existing aspect guides linked from each article. Those pages explain what a square or trine means in the tradition. The pair references have a narrower job: keeping the named bodies, chart ownership, examples and practical discussion questions together. A shared name for an angle does not erase the difference between one-chart and two-chart contexts.

## Choose a pair from a computed result

Begin with the compatibility tool if both participants want a comparison. Read a listed contact in full, including its chart roles and orb. The explanation link beside a supported result opens the matching geometry section. Selecting the result row continues to select its contact on the wheel; opening a reference is a separate action. The explanation does not need a copied name, location, timestamp or chart code.

Sun-Moon provides a vocabulary for deliberate direction alongside comfort and familiar responses. Moon-Venus places comfort beside appreciation and preferences. Moon-Mars places emotional pacing beside action and assertion. These are symbolic topics for an optional conversation. They do not assign permanent roles to the participants, and they are not tied to gender, relationship category or which person is drawn inside the wheel.

There is no requirement to find every pair in a result. A contact outside the implemented orb is omitted from the major-aspect list, and an unsupported body pair has no link in this small library. Neither absence is a verdict. The participants can discuss a useful question without first finding an angle that appears to justify it.

## Use an example without borrowing a story

Each pair article includes two engine-backed examples with explicit UTC timestamps. The fixtures are synthetic and do not describe actual people. One chart is held fixed while a different second instant provides a conjunction or a trine. This makes it possible to inspect the arithmetic without implying that a familiar public relationship proves an interpretation.

Read the example in this order: body ownership, timestamp, longitude, shortest separation, target angle and residual orb. Then identify which part is computed and which part is an editorial prompt. A replay verifies the longitudes and the aspect under the pinned engine version. It does not verify that two people would feel, behave or relate in a particular way. The schematic wraparound example separately explains why positions on either side of zero degrees can be close together.

## Keep missing times and consent visible

A noon reference is not a recovered birth time. The Moon changes longitude during a birth date, so an unknown time can change the displayed orb or the major aspect being listed. A precise-looking result must keep that limitation beside it. Do not invent a rising sign or houses from an assumed time. The individual birth chart tool offers a separate view of the supplied data and their limits.

Ask before entering another person's details, and ask again before sharing a comparison. A private invitation allows the recipient to choose whether to participate. Saving a chart or comparison is voluntary and uses the existing controls in the tools. Reading a reference requires neither an account nor a saved chart. No aspect reveals another person's feelings, supplies consent or overrides a stated boundary.

## A small task for the conversation

Choose one ordinary example before reading an interpretation: a request for help, a preferred pace for an activity or a change to a shared plan. Describe what each person actually asked for. Use the selected geometry as a way of arranging questions, then let either person correct the description or decline the exercise. If the symbolism makes the example less clear, leave the symbolism aside.

For an aspect within one person's chart, open the birth chart tool. That is the natal context and requires a distinct reading. This library stays with contacts between two charts and links only to available references. Its scope is deliberately limited to three pairs, supported calculation examples and practical questions that preserve each participant's own account of the exchange.
