Uranus was the first planet found with a telescope, spotted in 1781,
between the American revolution and the French one. Astrologers noticed
the company it arrived in, and have read it as the revolution planet ever
since.

That's the general pattern with the outer planets: the tradition takes
the discovery era as part of the meaning. For Uranus the portfolio is
disruption, independence, invention: lightning, both the bolt and the
idea. It's the planet assigned to whatever in you would rather be free
than comfortable, and to the moments when a long-stable arrangement ends
in an afternoon.

## What Uranus does to a chart

Wherever Uranus sits, the standard template doesn't take. Its house is
the department of your life that resists settling: the career that keeps
mutating, the living situation nobody else's parents understand, the
friendship circle assembled from seven incompatible worlds. The changes
there tend to be discontinuous, not drift but jump, the way a switch
differs from a dimmer.

Uranus rules [Aquarius](/aquarius/), a sign it shares custody of with
[Saturn](/learn/planets/saturn/), the traditional ruler. The pairing
sounds contradictory and isn't: Aquarius holds fixed principles about why
everything else should change. That's Uranian behavior in general, the
rebel with a system, the exit executed on schedule.

## Reading Uranus personally

Here's the part that matters for birth charts: Uranus spends about seven
years in a sign, so its sign belongs to your whole age cohort. Everyone
born in the same stretch shares it, and it describes a generation's style
of breaking rules, useful for history, useless for distinguishing you
from your classmates. The personal information lives in the house, which
only your birth time can place, and in aspects to your inner planets.
Uranus in [conjunction](/learn/aspects/conjunction/) with your Moon or in
a [square](/learn/aspects/square/) to your Venus is not generational at
all. That's a private lightning rod, and its owners can usually describe
the weather.

[Run your chart](/birth-chart/) and check the house first. A
seventh-house Uranus and a tenth-house Uranus belong to the same cohort
and live in different novels.

## The 84-year lap

Uranus circles the zodiac in 84 years, seven per sign, one lap per
lifetime if the lifetime cooperates. Its retrogrades run about five
months of every year, so routine that the tradition barely comments;
with the outer planets, the aspect being made matters far more than the
direction of travel.

The lap divides into quarter-marks that land near ages 21, 42, 63, and
84: a square, an opposition, a square, and the return, each one a
scheduled argument between the life you've assembled and the person
assembling it.

The famous checkpoint is the halfway mark: around age 42, transiting
Uranus opposes its own natal position, and every freedom postponed since
adolescence comes up for review. Astrology's midlife
transit predates the midlife-crisis cliché by some centuries and is more
generous than the cliché. The honest question it poses isn't "is this
all there is?" but "which of these constraints did I actually choose?"
Handled well, the answer rearranges a life without wrecking it. The
return itself waits at 84, when the planet of surprises completes the
one journey it always finishes on time.
