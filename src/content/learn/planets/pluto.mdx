Astronomy demoted Pluto in 2006. Astrology declined to follow, not out
of stubbornness, exactly, but because the tradition's categories were
never about mass. In a chart, Pluto had spent seventy years describing
something specific, and a vote in Prague didn't change what.

What it describes is the underworld portfolio: power, obsession, secrets,
endings, and the rebuilding that follows them. Pluto was discovered in
1930, as depth psychology was mapping the unconscious and physics was a
decade from splitting the atom, and the tradition, which reads discovery
eras as part of an outer planet's meaning, took the hint twice over.
This is the planet of what's buried, and of how much energy is stored
there.

## What Pluto governs

Transformation, in the demolition-and-rebuild sense rather than the
makeover sense. Wherever Pluto sits in a birth chart, surfaces don't
hold: that house is a part of life where you periodically lose the
structure you built and discover which parts were load-bearing. It's
also where your relationship to power lives: having it, hiding it,
handing it over, or spending a decade retrieving it.

Pluto rules [Scorpio](/scorpio/), the sign that treats trust as a vault
rather than a lobby. Traditionally Scorpio belonged to
[Mars](/learn/planets/mars/), and the two rulerships split the sign's
method cleanly: Mars is the strike, Pluto is the patience before it.
People with strong Pluto or Scorpio placements tend to know this about
themselves, usually earlier than they can say it out loud.

## Reading Pluto personally

Pluto's sign is the least personal fact about it. With 12 to 31 years
per sign, a Pluto sign describes a generation's shared underworld, what
an entire cohort is compelled to dig up and transform. Pluto in Scorpio
(roughly 1983 to 1995) marks one such cohort; Pluto in Leo another. For
the individual reading, look at the house, which requires a birth time,
and at aspects to your inner planets. Pluto in an
[opposition](/learn/aspects/opposition/) to your Moon or a
[conjunction](/learn/aspects/conjunction/) with your Sun is not a
generational statement. It's a private intensity, and its owners are
rarely surprised to hear about it.

[Run your chart](/birth-chart/) and find Pluto's house. That's the room
with the locked cabinet, and the reliable pattern is that the cabinet
opens a few times per lifetime whether you have the key or not. The
useful preparation is knowing which room it's in.

## The longest cycle in the chart

Pluto takes about 248 years to lap the zodiac, on an orbit eccentric
enough that the per-sign pace swings from a dozen years to over thirty.
Between 1979 and 1999 it was actually inside
[Neptune's](/learn/planets/neptune/) orbit, sprinting (by its standards)
through Scorpio. It retrogrades five to six months of every year, which
with a mover this slow is texture rather than news.

No one lives to see a Pluto return. Nations do: the United States had
its first in 2022, when transiting Pluto reached the degree of Capricorn
it held in 1776, an event astrologers had circled on the calendar for
generations, on the theory that even a country eventually gets asked
what its power is for. For a person, Pluto's schedule is simpler and
stranger: one partial lap, a handful of exact contacts, and the standing
observation that the chart's slowest planet is the one whose work, once
done, stays done.
