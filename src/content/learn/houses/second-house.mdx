Once the [first house](/learn/houses/first-house/) has settled who is
arriving, the chart turns to logistics: what you own, what you earn, and
what you would defend if pressed. That is the second house, the territory
of resources, and of the values that decide which resources count.

## What the second house covers

The traditional list is blunt: money, movable possessions, livelihood.
Money here is the earned kind (the paycheck, the invoice, the craft that
converts hours into rent) as opposed to the inheritances and joint
accounts that belong to the [eighth house](/learn/houses/eighth-house/),
directly across the chart. Possessions likewise mean the things you
actually use and keep: the tools, the good coat, whatever would be missed
if it went.

Underneath the ledger sits the more interesting layer: values. What you
will pay for without flinching and what always feels like waste; which
work seems beneath you and which feels like yours; the exact point where
"I could" becomes "I won't." And there is the matter of reliability, what
you lean on when the ground shifts. For some charts that's a number in an
account; for others it's a trade, a garden, or a person who always answers
the phone. The second house names your ballast, whatever form it takes.

## The sign on the cusp

The second house follows immediately after the ascendant, so its cusp
depends on your birth time. No clock, no cusps. A
[birth chart](/birth-chart/) run with even an approximate time will place
it, along with the eleven other houses.

The sign there describes your default relationship with material life.
Taurus on the cusp favors steady accumulation, comfort as policy, and a
reliable instinct for what lasts. Gemini tends toward plural income and portable
skills; Capricorn budgets like an engineer and relaxes at sixty; Pisces
loses the receipts and somehow manages anyway. The cusp is a style, not a
verdict: it says how you handle what you have, not how much of it there
will be.

## Planets in the second house

A planet here brings its agenda to your accounts. [Venus](/learn/planets/venus/)
earns through taste and relationships and spends on beauty without
apology. Mars earns fast, spends faster, and treats income as a contact
sport. Saturn usually starts out with scarcity, real or vividly imagined,
and builds toward a late, durable competence with money that nothing can
rattle. Jupiter attracts resources generously and redistributes them
almost as quickly, sometimes on the walk home from receiving them. The
Moon ties security to mood and mood to security: groceries function as
self-care, and the savings account doubles as a nervous system.

Several planets here make material life a major plot line: earning,
keeping, and valuing become the arena where the rest of the chart gets
worked out. An empty second house, meanwhile, is the common case and means
nothing ominous. The cusp sign and its ruler run the territory alone,
usually with less drama, which most budgets prefer.
