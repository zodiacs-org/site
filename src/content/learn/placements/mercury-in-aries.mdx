The answer is out before the question has finished landing. [Mercury](/learn/glossary/#mercury) in
Aries runs the shortest route in the zodiac between a thought and a
sentence, and it does not always check for traffic.

## How it shows up

[Mercury](/learn/planets/mercury/) is the chart’s information
department: what you notice, how you sort it, what you say. In
[Aries](/aries/), the department is all delivery. Thoughts arrive hot
and get spoken as first drafts, repaired later if repair turns out to
be needed. This is a mind that learns by touching the stove: manuals
unread, buttons pressed, the whole thing understood cold by the third
broken attempt. Ask for directions and you get “left at the light,
you’ll see it,” which works, provided you also drive like you mean it.

The texting style gives it away: a burst of five short lines while
you’re still composing one, each sent the moment it existed. So does
the meeting behavior: first with the objection, first with the joke,
first to name the flaw everyone else was circling politely. Podcasts
play at double speed here; so do apologies.

## How it argues

Out loud, briefly, and without an archive. Mercury in Aries says the
sharp thing to your face, never in the group chat afterward, and it is
genuinely confused by grudges assembled from unsent messages. The
flare is fast and the reset is faster: an hour later the fight is over
and filed, which can insult someone still bleeding from it. Winning
the opening exchange comes naturally; sieges do not. If an argument
outlasts its attention, it will concede the details just to keep
moving. Check what you actually won before framing it.

At work this is who you want in the incident channel: quick read,
quick call, comfortable being wrong out loud in front of everyone,
which is rarer and more useful than being right quietly. The tax shows
up in long documents, slow consensus, and any meeting that should have
been a sentence.

## The growth edge

The second thought. The first take here is fast and often right; the
second take is better and almost never consulted. Building a small
delay into the machinery (the beat before hitting send, the reread
this placement finds physically boring) is worth more than another
point of speed, because every fast miss spends credibility the fast
hits earned. The other work is patience with slower processors: some
people who pause aren’t stalling, they’re right slowly, and talking
over them costs information.

The sky schedules the delay three times a year anyway. The
[Mercury retrograde](/mercury-retrograde/) calendar lists the exact
review windows. Treat them as maintenance for the send button, and
run your [birth chart](/birth-chart/) to see which house takes the
fast talk.
