[Venus](/learn/glossary/#venus) in Leo loves the way a theater lights its stage, fully, warmly,
with no apology for the wattage. If you’re wondering whether this
person likes you, they don’t. When they do, you’ll know, and so will
everyone else.

## How it shows up

What [Venus](/learn/planets/venus/) wants (pleasure, beauty,
affection), [Leo](/leo/) wants witnessed. Love here is declared and
produced: the reservation with a plan behind it, the gift wrapped like
it mattered, the toast made in front of the whole table. Attraction
runs toward shine (confidence, talent, people who occupy their own
lives fully) and taste follows: one dramatic piece over five safe
ones, the coat that arrives slightly before you do. Spending is
ceremonial. Checks get picked up, birthdays are produced rather than
observed, and the splurge on a loved one never needs justifying.

Call it production value in the honest sense: the love is real *and*
it wants witnesses. A compliment paid in private is, to this Venus,
only half paid.

## In love

The gift is wholeheartedness. You are not merely liked by this
placement. You are chosen, backed, bragged about, and defended in
rooms you’re not in. Your wins get celebrated harder than its own,
your bad days get met with warmth instead of analysis, and the
romance refuses to fade into logistics: anniversaries stay events,
and ordinary Tuesdays keep getting upgraded without warning.

The price is that the spotlight must swing both ways. Affection that
goes unacknowledged curdles; criticism, however gentle, can land as
betrayal; and sulking, when it comes, is staged at the same scale as
the devotion. The relationship is also expected to be admirable, not
merely good, a standard that actual life, with its laundry and its
head colds, keeps failing to meet.

## The growth edge

The work is loving with the house lights off: no audience, no
narration, no proof. The mature version discovers that being known
beats being applauded: the partner who saw the unbrushed, unfunny,
unspotlit version and stayed is giving a better review than any room
ever will. It also learns to hand over the stage some nights and
enjoy the show from the seats, which turns out to be its own kind of
starring role.
