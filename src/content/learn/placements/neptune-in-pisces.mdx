Only one sign is [Neptune](/learn/glossary/#neptune)’s own. Modern astrology assigns it Pisces
(Jupiter ruled the sign first, and the older claim still counts), so the
generation born from 2012 to 2025 got the planet undiluted. Even at
home the rule holds: Neptune spends about 14 years in a sign and
describes a generation, its ideal and its blur and its dream, leaving
the house and aspects to say what one person makes of it.

## How it shows up

[Neptune](/learn/planets/neptune/) in [Pisces](/pisces/) is imagination
in its native element: boundless, compassionate, hard to pin to a
schedule. This cohort, born 2012 through 2025,
grew up with the border between real and imagined kept deliberately
soft: screens that talk back, stories that continue across every
device, friends who exist and friends who half do. Their shared ideal
is empathy itself, felt weather-wide; their shared reflex is to treat
the imagined as one more place a person can live.

Neptune first touched Pisces in April 2011, settled in from February
2012, and left on March 30, 2025, with one last pass ending January 26,
2026.

## The fog at home

In its own sign, Neptune’s glamour and its fog pour from the same tap.
The romanticized figure is the feeler: the artist, the healer, the
friend who absorbs everyone’s weather without checking the forecast.
The blur is exactly that porousness: compassion without a membrane,
escape as a first resort, real difficulty locating where the self ends
and the atmosphere begins. Earlier cohorts learned boundaries young and
dreamed later; this one arrived at full flood and will spend adulthood
building the levees.

## In your own chart

For this generation the house is everything: it says where the flood
concentrates. A twelfth-house Neptune in Pisces needs solitude the way
other people need sleep; a second-house one idealizes having enough and
blurs what enough means. Aspects to the Sun or Moon show which children
carry the era as personality rather than backdrop.
[Run a birth chart](/birth-chart/) to place it. With a cohort this
porous, the specific beats the general every time.
