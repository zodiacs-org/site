Eleven signs are places [Uranus](/learn/glossary/#uranus) visits; Aquarius is the address modern
astrology lists as its own, with Saturn holding the older title. The
planet was last home from January 1996 to March 2003, after a first
crossing in spring 1995, with a final pass ending in December 2003.

## How it shows up

[Uranus](/learn/planets/uranus/) holds a sign for roughly seven years,
which makes the sign a generational marker, the cohort’s shared theory
of rebellion, while the house and aspects say what any one chart does
with it. In [Aquarius](/aquarius/), the theory is systemic: don’t storm
the institution, don’t even argue with it; build the network that makes
it unnecessary.

This is the generation born from 1996 to 2003, and its native territory
is the group: friendship at scale, alliances indifferent to geography,
the collective as something you assemble rather than get assigned.

## What this generation redesigns

Belonging itself. Where older cohorts joined what existed (the club,
the congregation, the company), this one drafts its own charters:
communities convened around an interest, dissolved and reformed without
ceremony, run on rules the members wrote. The Aquarian paradox arrives
doubled here: firm principles about staying flexible, tight groups
organized around independence.

The cost is distance. A generation fluent in the collective can go
vague on the particular: everyone connected, no one close, the crowd
easier to love than the person in it. The mature version keeps the
network and learns the difference between a signal and a hand on the
shoulder.

## In your own chart

Born between 1996 and 2003, you share the sign with your entire
cohort; what distinguishes you is the house (the eleventh doubles the
signature, the seventh runs the redesign inside partnership) and any
close aspects to your inner planets. Run your
[birth chart](/birth-chart/) to see where the home-sign fluency
actually lives in your life. The placement is generational; its street
address is yours.
