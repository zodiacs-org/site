Venus-Mars aspects in a birth chart describe the angle between Venus and Mars at the moment a person was born. This is a natal reading: both positions come from one chart and one instant, and the aspect describes something within one person. In modern Western astrology Venus stands for value and pleasure, what a person finds attractive, beautiful or worth keeping, and Mars stands for drive, how they assert themselves, pursue what they want and handle anger. The aspect describes how wanting and going after it meet.

This pair behaves differently from the Moon pairs. Venus and Mars both move slowly, Venus about a degree a day and Mars rather less, so the angle between them changes by around half a degree a day. In the site's eight-year sample a Venus-Mars aspect stayed listed for between 9 and 54 consecutive days. Many people born in the same few weeks share it. That makes the birth hour almost irrelevant here, and it also means the aspect is less personal than a Moon aspect: it describes a shared background that each chart's signs, houses and other aspects make individual.

Venus and Mars are often read together when people talk about attraction, and the sections below touch on that. They describe one person's own way of combining value and effort, in work and play as much as in affection. They do not forecast anything about anyone's relationships. Read them as prompts and keep what fits.

<h2 id="conjunction">Venus conjunct Mars in a birth chart</h2>

A Venus-Mars conjunction places the two planets close together on the wheel, usually in the same sign. In the sky this happens when both are seen in the same direction, often near the Sun, and it is the longest-lasting of the five angles for this pair: in the site's sample a conjunction stayed within its 8 degree orb for between four and eight weeks. A good number of people share it.

The traditional reading joins value and drive in one place. What a person finds attractive and how they go after it tend to be the same impulse; liking something and moving toward it happen at once. Many astrologers describe this as a lively, warm placement, sometimes an artistic one, since care for how things look and energy to make them can combine. That is a tradition's description, not a measure of anyone's talent.

A concrete example. Someone loves a particular craft and works at it with real intensity; the pleasure and the effort feel like one thing. That fits the conjunction. A useful question is what happens when the two separate, when a task must be done that the person does not enjoy, or when something they enjoy asks for no effort at all. With the conjunction, either case can feel oddly flat.

The tradition also links this aspect to directness about what one wants. That can be refreshing, and it can make it hard to want something quietly. Notice whether wanting tends to turn into action before there has been time to consider it, and whether that suits the situation. The aspect describes a tendency; the person decides what to do with it.

The sign and house matter a great deal, since both planets share them. A conjunction in Taurus reads differently from one in Aries or Scorpio, and the house says where the combined impulse tends to show up. A close conjunction is usually given more weight than one near the 8 degree limit. Because this aspect holds for weeks, its exact orb, more than its presence, is what distinguishes one chart from another born nearby.

<h2 id="sextile">Venus sextile Mars in a birth chart</h2>

A sextile puts Venus and Mars about 60 degrees apart, two signs from each other. The signs usually cooperate, fire with air or earth with water, while staying distinct. In the site's sample a Venus-Mars sextile lasted from about a week and a half to six weeks, depending on how the two planets were moving relative to each other at the time.

The traditional reading calls the sextile an opportunity. Value and drive are separate enough to stay distinct and similar enough to assist each other. A person with this aspect may find that effort is easier when it serves something they care about, and that what they care about tends to give them reasons to act. The tradition adds that an opportunity takes a little initiative to use.

Picture someone who enjoys cooking for friends. The sextile suggests a practical joining of the two functions: the effort of shopping and preparing becomes easier because of the pleasure at the end, and the pleasure is sharper because effort went into it. Nothing about this is dramatic. The reflection is where such small joinings already happen, and whether the person would like more of them.

The sextile is the smallest of the five major angles, and for this pair the site uses the standard 4 degree orb, narrower than the 5 degrees allowed when the Sun or Moon is involved. That makes a Venus-Mars sextile a fairly exacting aspect. A chart that shows none may have Venus and Mars just outside it, and the two longitudes in the planet table show how close they came.

There is a habit of calling sextiles good and stopping there. A mild aspect is easy to miss. Someone may not recognize a Venus-Mars sextile until they notice how often enjoyment and effort already work together for them, in small ways. Ask where that happens now rather than whether the aspect is fortunate. The answer belongs to the person, and the description is only a starting point.

<h2 id="square">Venus square Mars in a birth chart</h2>

A square places Venus and Mars about 90 degrees apart, three signs from each other. The signs usually share a mode, cardinal, fixed or mutable, and differ in element. They act in a similar manner for different reasons. In the site's sample a Venus-Mars square held for between about two and five weeks, so this aspect, too, is shared by many people born around the same time.

The traditional reading calls the square a tension. What a person values and how they go after it do not always agree. They may want harmony and find themselves pushing, or want to push and find themselves holding back to keep things pleasant. The tradition does not treat this as a defect. It describes a recurring negotiation between ease and effort that tends to come up in ordinary choices.

A concrete case. Someone values calm, considerate exchanges and also has a quick temper when something feels unfair. Both are real. The square describes two impulses that tend to meet in ordinary disagreements, at work or at home, and suggests that a practical arrangement helps more than a resolution to be one way: a pause before replying, a habit of saying what is wanted plainly, a place for energy that is not a conversation.

Astrologers often describe Venus-Mars squares as a source of creative or competitive energy, since the friction has to go somewhere. That is a reading worth testing against experience, not a promise. Some people with this square notice it strongly; others hardly at all. It describes tension inside one person's wanting and pursuing. It does not describe how attractive anyone is or how any relationship goes.

The site counts a square within 7 degrees for this pair. A close square is usually given more weight than a wide one. Because Venus and Mars move slowly, the birth hour almost never changes this aspect, but the planets' signs and houses differ from chart to chart and do most of the work of making a shared square individual. Read those before deciding what the square is about.

<h2 id="trine">Venus trine Mars in a birth chart</h2>

A trine places Venus and Mars about 120 degrees apart, four signs from each other and usually in the same element. The two planets share a temperament, fire with fire or water with water, so value and drive speak a similar language. In the site's sample a Venus-Mars trine lasted from about nine days to nearly five weeks.

The traditional reading calls the trine harmonious. What a person enjoys and how they pursue it tend to agree, and acting on a preference may feel natural rather than forced. People with this aspect are sometimes described as relaxed about what they want: they go after it without much internal argument. The tradition's usual caution applies. Ease is a condition, not an accomplishment, and it can be left unused.

Consider someone who plays a sport for the pleasure of it and trains without needing to push themselves; effort and enjoyment arrive together. That is the trine at its plainest. A useful reflection is what happens when a goal requires effort without pleasure, or pleasure that asks for patience. Someone used to the two lining up may find the gap unfamiliar. Noticing that is useful; expecting it is not.

Astrologers sometimes contrast the trine with the square. The square keeps asking for adjustment, while the trine rarely asks for anything. That can make the trine quiet in a chart with louder aspects. It is often read as a background ease in how a person combines effort and enjoyment, helpful but easy to overlook. Look at the orb before giving it much weight.

The site counts a trine within 7 degrees for this pair. The birth-time section below shows a Venus-Mars trine near that limit which the calculator lists for only part of its UTC date. That is the one situation in which a birth time matters for this pair: an aspect close to the edge of its orb. Otherwise, a trine listed for one hour of a birth day is listed for all of it.

<h2 id="opposition">Venus opposite Mars in a birth chart</h2>

An opposition places Venus and Mars about 180 degrees apart, on opposite sides of the wheel. Because Venus stays near the Sun, this puts Mars far from the Sun too, often near its own opposition, when Mars is closest to the Earth and brightest in the night sky. It is also the least frequent of the five angles for this pair: the site's eight-year sample contains four opposition windows, each lasting ten days to two weeks.

The traditional reading treats the opposition as awareness through contrast. Value and drive face each other, so a person may come to know what they want by noticing how it differs from how they act, and the other way round. That can feel like being pulled between harmony and assertion. It can also feel like perspective: a clear sense of when to yield and when to press.

A practical example. Someone is accommodating in most things and then, at a certain point, firmly not. Both sides are genuine, and they may alternate rather than blend. The opposition invites balance rather than a final choice between them. A useful reflection is whether the switch between the two feels chosen or abrupt, and what tends to trigger it.

Astrologers often say oppositions are met through other people: a person may first recognize one side of their own opposition in someone close to them. Treat that as a suggestion to test, not a rule. It describes how one person notices their own wanting and pursuing. It does not say whom anyone is drawn to, or how a relationship goes.

The site counts an opposition within 8 degrees for this pair, the standard limit rather than the wider one used for the Sun and Moon. As with every Venus-Mars angle, the birth hour rarely matters. The planets' signs and houses, and any close aspects they make to other planets, do more to say what a particular opposition is about than the angle alone.

## Reading Venus-Mars in your own chart

Start with what the calculator shows: the angle and the exact orb. For this pair the birth time is a minor question, unless the orb is close to its limit. The more useful question is what the aspect shares with everyone born that month and what is particular to this chart.

The particular part comes from signs, houses and other aspects. Venus in its sign and house describes what kind of thing a person values and where; Mars in its sign and house describes how and where they act. A close aspect from the Moon or the Sun to either planet makes the theme more personal than the Venus-Mars angle alone.

Finally, keep the description and the person separate. These readings suggest patterns in how one person combines enjoyment and effort. They are not statements about anyone's partners, and they do not forecast attraction or its outcome. A useful reading leaves room for "not quite", and for the person's own better description.
