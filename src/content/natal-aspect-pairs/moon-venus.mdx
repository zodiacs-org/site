Moon-Venus aspects in a birth chart describe the angle between the Moon and Venus at the moment a person was born. This is a natal reading: both positions come from one chart and one instant, and the aspect describes something within one person. In modern Western astrology the Moon stands for habit and comfort, what a person reaches for when they are tired or unsure, and Venus stands for taste and value, what they find pleasing, beautiful or worth keeping close. The aspect describes how the two meet.

The two bodies move at very different speeds. The Moon moves about 13 degrees a day through the zodiac, and about 12 compared with Venus, which moves about one. So it is almost always the Moon that makes or breaks a Moon-Venus aspect, and the birth time matters as much here as for Sun-Moon. Venus also never strays more than about 47 degrees from the Sun, so a Moon-Venus angle tends to fall near the matching Moon phase: an opposition, for instance, usually belongs to a birth near the full Moon.

The Moon and Venus are often read together when people talk about affection. The sections below stay with one person's own comforts and tastes.

<h2 id="conjunction">Moon conjunct Venus in a birth chart</h2>

A Moon-Venus conjunction places the two bodies close together on the wheel, usually in the same sign. Because Venus is never far from the Sun, this tends to happen within a few days of the new Moon.

The traditional reading brings comfort and taste into one place. What soothes the person and what they find beautiful tend to be the same things: a particular kind of room, a style of food, a piece of music played often. Comfort and pleasure can also be hard to tell apart when they share a degree.

Someone keeps their home carefully and enjoys doing it; arranging things is both how they relax and how they show what they like. That fits the conjunction. The question worth asking is what happens when the familiar and the pleasing come apart, when a new taste is not yet comfortable or a comfortable habit no longer pleases.

The tradition often links this aspect to wanting things to be pleasant for everyone present. That can be generous, or it can mean smoothing things over too early. The sign and house shape the conjunction more than the angle does, since both bodies share them. With an unknown birth time, check the birth-time panel below: the Moon can move far enough in a few hours to bring a conjunction inside the 10 degree limit or take it out.

<h2 id="sextile">Moon sextile Venus in a birth chart</h2>

A sextile puts the Moon and Venus about 60 degrees apart, two signs from each other. With Venus close to the Sun, this usually happens in the week or so either side of the new Moon, while the Moon is a crescent or nearing a quarter. The signs involved tend to cooperate, fire with air or earth with water, while remaining distinct.

The traditional reading calls the sextile an opportunity. Comfort and taste are different enough to stay separate and similar enough to help each other. Small pleasures can support a person's routines, and routines can leave room for pleasure, when they arrange things that way. An opportunity takes some initiative to use.

Picture someone who finds weekday evenings flat. The sextile suggests a small, practical answer: a regular meal cooked with care, flowers on the table, a standing call with a friend. Each joins something soothing with something enjoyed. Do such arrangements already exist?

The sextile is the smallest of the five major angles, and the site counts it within 5 degrees when the Moon is involved. A Moon-Venus sextile can come and go within a day, and the birth-time panel below shows one that holds for only part of its date. A mild aspect is also easy to overlook: ask where comfort and pleasure already work together, rather than whether the aspect is lucky.

<h2 id="square">Moon square Venus in a birth chart</h2>

A square places the Moon and Venus about 90 degrees apart, three signs from each other. Because Venus stays near the Sun, this angle tends to fall within a few days of a quarter Moon. The signs involved usually share a mode: cardinal, fixed or mutable. They differ in element, so the two functions act in a similar manner for different reasons.

The traditional reading calls the square a tension. What comforts a person and what they find pleasing can pull in different directions: a familiar habit may not be one they like, and something they enjoy may not settle them. It describes a recurring negotiation between need and taste that turns up in ordinary choices.

Someone loves bright, busy places and enjoys going out, yet comes home from those evenings drained and in need of quiet. The square does not decide between the two; it describes a question that tends to return until it gets a practical answer, such as shorter evenings, a quiet day afterwards or an outing that offers both.

Some astrologers read this square as restlessness, others as a sharp sense of taste. The site counts a square within 8 degrees when the Moon is involved. With an unknown time the angle can move about 12 degrees across the day, so a square read from noon may have been tighter, wider or absent at the real hour.

<h2 id="trine">Moon trine Venus in a birth chart</h2>

A trine places the Moon and Venus about 120 degrees apart, four signs from each other and usually in the same element. With Venus near the Sun, this tends to fall between a quarter Moon and the full Moon, in the gibbous days when the Moon is more than half lit. The two bodies share a temperament, earth with earth or air with air, so comfort and taste speak a similar language.

The traditional reading calls the trine harmonious. What settles a person and what they enjoy tend to agree, and moving between rest and pleasure may feel unforced. The tradition adds that ease is a condition, not an accomplishment, and it can be taken for granted.

Consider someone whose weekends restore them without much planning: a walk they like, a meal they enjoy, an evening with people who are comfortable to be with. That is the trine at its plainest. A useful reflection is what happens when a move or a change of routine interrupts that agreement.

Next to the square, which keeps asking for adjustment, the trine rarely asks for anything, so it can go unnoticed. A close Moon-Venus trine is often read as a steady background. The site counts a trine within 8 degrees when the Moon is involved. Trines and sextiles are often grouped together, but they are different angles with different orbs.

<h2 id="opposition">Moon opposite Venus in a birth chart</h2>

An opposition places the Moon and Venus about 180 degrees apart, on opposite sides of the wheel. Since Venus stays near the Sun, this usually belongs to a birth within a few days of the full Moon, when the Moon rises around sunset and Venus is low near the Sun. The signs involved sit across from each other and are often read as complementary.

The traditional reading treats the opposition as awareness through contrast. Comfort and taste face each other, so a person may come to know each one by its difference from the other. What they reach for when tired is not always what they find beautiful.

Someone enjoys elegant, deliberate surroundings but relaxes best in a worn, familiar room, and moves between the two. A useful reflection is whether that movement feels chosen or whether one side keeps losing ground.

Astrologers often say that a person first recognizes one side of an opposition in someone they spend time with. That is a suggestion from the tradition, and it casts no one in a role. The site counts an opposition within 10 degrees when the Moon is involved, as wide as the conjunction. ## Reading Moon-Venus in your own chart

Start with what the calculator shows: the angle, the exact orb and whether the birth time is known. For this pair the birth time comes first. If it is unknown, look at the birth-time panel and decide how much weight a noon aspect can bear. A tight one usually survives; a loose one may not.

Then look at the signs and houses. The Moon's sign and house describe what kind of comfort a person seeks and where; Venus's do the same for pleasure and value.

These readings describe a pattern in one person's preferences to reflect on, not a verdict on anyone or a forecast of what happens. A useful reading leaves room for "that is not quite me", and for the person's own better description.
