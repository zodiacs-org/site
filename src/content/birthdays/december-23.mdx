The solstice is behind you and the question is settled: December 23
is [Capricorn](/capricorn/) in every year on record here, no table
required. These are the sign's opening degrees, Saturn's own decan
starting Saturn's own sign, and birthdays here get Capricorn at its
newest: the year just turned, the light just starting its slow climb
back, the whole ascent still ahead.

## First days of the builder

A [Capricorn Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-capricorn/) this early in
the sign carries cardinal drive. It initiates. Forget the stereotype
of the cautious plodder: December 23 starts things, projects,
households, five-year plans drafted at twenty-two. What makes it
Capricorn rather than Aries is the material. This date starts
foundations, not fireworks; the first move is always the load-bearing
one, the apprenticeship before the shop. People born today are often
the youngest-ever something in their family or workplace, and quietly
annoyed the record took that long to fall.

## Slow to start, built to stay

Love moves at a surveyor's pace: December 23 checks the ground before
building on it, which partners can misread as coolness. It is not.
It is the same seriousness that will later re-roof the house in a
weekend without being asked. Once committed, this date is
weatherproof: the one who stays through the flood year, whose
promises carry no expiration date. At work it wants the long arc:
entry at the bottom, mastery by the middle, and something standing at
the end with its fingerprints all through the mortar.

## Seriousness, early onset

The friction: gravity arrives decades before it is required. Young
December 23s can skip their own youth (working through the summers
everyone else spent being nineteen), and midlife sometimes sends a
bill for the fun that got deferred. The growth edge, kindly: the
climb is real, but it was never a punishment. Schedule a few of the
summit's pleasures at base camp and the whole route improves.
