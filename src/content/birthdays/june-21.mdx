The June solstice is the moment the Sun leaves [Gemini](/gemini/) and
enters [Cancer](/cancer/), and in most years since 1940 that moment
falls inside June 21 itself. No date in the month depends on the
clock like this one: two babies born the same June 21, morning and
night, can carry different Sun signs. A minority of years give the
whole day to Gemini, a smaller minority to Cancer. The table above
sorts yours, and if it shows a split, the hour is everything.

## The hinge temperament

People born at a sign boundary usually describe themselves in both
columns, and here the columns genuinely rhyme. The
[Gemini Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-gemini/) ends its run
distilled: verbal, connective, curious to the last degree. The
[Cancer Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-cancer/) opens at full
instinct: protective, tuned to rooms, already keeping people. The
June 21 blend tends to read as warmth with commentary, someone who
notices what you need and can also say it better than you could.

## Bright company, deep loyalty

In love, the mix is disarming: the playfulness of late spring, the
stickiness of early summer. This birthday banters like air and
attaches like water, and partners sometimes take a year to notice
how deep the roots went while everyone was laughing. At work, it
suits roles where communication carries care: teaching, editing
with a mentor's hand, running the team whose birthdays it knows.
Cold brilliance for its own sake leaves it hollow fast.

## Say it or store it

The friction: two processing systems, each grabbing the wheel. The
Gemini reflex narrates a hurt into a story; the Cancer reflex files
it away silently for later. Either way, the person who caused it
never hears the plain version. The growth is choosing the third
option early: the direct sentence, spoken once, while it is still
small enough to say kindly.
