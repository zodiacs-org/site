Two weeks past the equinox, the light is committed to leaving and the
year starts keeping only what holds. October 6 suits the moment:
mid-[Libra](/libra/), Saturn decan, the part of the sign that builds
agreements meant to survive winters. Where early-decan Libra makes
the peace, this stretch maintains it, less spark, more mortar.

## Built to outlast the season

Durability is the value under everything here. This date distrusts
the beautiful arrangement that can't take weight and would rather
have the honest structure than the pretty one. Friendships run long
(decades, often) because upkeep is instinctive: the check-in call,
the standing dinner that never quite lapses, the favor returned
before it was consciously owed.

## Slow yes, long hold

The yes arrives late and means everything after that. Partners get
constancy that doesn't need restating, plus a quiet allergy to drama
that keeps the household weather mild. A
[Libra Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-libra/) in this decan works best
on long horizons too: institutions, infrastructure, a body of work
assembled brick by brick, the satisfaction of maintaining something
excellent over launching something loud. Reference letters written
about people born today tend to contain the word steady.

## Rules outliving their reasons

The friction is fidelity to forms after their purpose has left: the
tradition nobody enjoys anymore, the process defended because it is
the process. The growth move is an annual audit of your own rules.
Keep the ones still carrying weight, thank and retire the rest.
Loyalty belongs to people, not to formats.

Charm has a schedule it keeps. Whose doors it calls at first (partners, publics, kin) is written by your birth hour; the [calculator](/birth-chart/) reads the visiting list without hurry.
