Born on December 17, you get the [Sagittarius](/sagittarius/) that
cannot keep what it learns. Every cut of this sign collects (places,
books, mistakes, philosophies), but in the Sun's own decan the
collection wants students. Knowledge that stops with this date feels
unfinished to it, like a joke told to an empty kitchen.

## Experience, passed on

The [Sagittarius Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-sagittarius/) here has
the classic teacher's double gift: genuine substance and the
shamelessness to perform it. December 17 people explain things a beat
before being asked (how the wine is made, why the empire fell, what
the error message actually means) and mostly get away with it
because the delivery is warm and the material is good. Their favorite
compliment is not "you're brilliant." It is "I still use what you
showed me."

## The classroom everywhere

In love, this date teaches and, when it is healthy, learns: couples
describe whole eras named after each other's enthusiasms, the
climbing years, the bread years. The generous version makes a partner
feel capable; the unexamined version makes them feel corrected. Work
hardly needs the paragraph: teaching, training, coaching, editing,
medicine, any craft with apprentices. Even in unrelated jobs,
December 17 becomes the unofficial faculty, the one new hires get
sent to, the author of the one document everyone actually reads.

## Preaching past the bell

The friction, kindly put: not every moment is teachable. A partner
venting wants company, not curriculum; a friend mid-mistake sometimes
needs to finish making it. December 17's growth edge is telling a
student apart from a person having a day, and remembering that
silence, occasionally, is the advanced course. The best teachers this
date ever had knew when to close the book, too.

The lesson plan is solar and set. The classroom assignment (home, stage, clinic, page) is written by the hour you were born, and the [full chart](/birth-chart/) posts it.
