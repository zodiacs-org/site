Of all the dates a birthday can land on, March 20 sits closest to the
zodiac's own new year: the March equinox, where the Sun leaves
[Pisces](/pisces/) and enters [Aries](/aries/) at the wheel's zero
point. In most years since 1940 that crossing happens during this
very date (a morning birth comes out Pisces, a late one Aries),
while in the remaining years the whole day stays Pisces. One thing
never happens: a March 20 that is Aries from midnight. The table
above holds your year's answer.

## Last water, first fire

The two signs meeting here could hardly differ more, which is what
makes the blend interesting. The
[Pisces Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-pisces/) closes the zodiac:
receptive, boundary-blurring, wise about endings. The
[Aries Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-aries/) opens it: direct,
ignition-minded, certain the interesting part is ahead. People born
on this hinge often report both settings installed, a deep reader of
rooms who nonetheless moves first, a dreamer with a starting pistol.
The mix can look inconsistent from outside; from inside it feels like
exhale, then inhale.

## How the mix loves and works

In relationships the blend is tender and abrupt by turns: Piscean
attunement through the quiet stretches, Aries candor when something
must change now. Partners get empathy that can suddenly grow teeth,
clarifying, if occasionally startling. Work-wise this boundary excels
at beginnings that require feel: launching things people have to
love, reviving demoralized teams, art with a ship date. Pure
maintenance and pure combat both fit badly.

## The whiplash to manage

The friction is internal tempo. The Pisces half wants to absorb and
wait; the Aries half wanted to act an hour ago. Unmanaged, the
alternation reads as hot-and-cold to everyone else, three weeks of
drift, then a lurch of decisions. The steadier pattern is sequencing
on purpose: feel first, then move once, cleanly, instead of
oscillating.
