By March 4 the Sun is pushing toward the heart of [Pisces](/pisces/),
still inside the Moon's decan by the old reckoning, and if the Moon
means anything here, it means memory. This is the Pisces that keeps
things: friendships from age six, ticket stubs, the exact words
someone used fifteen Novembers ago, both the kind ones and the other
kind.

## A long memory in a fluid sign

Pisces is mutable water, the current that takes any shape, which
makes the March 4 constancy a genuine surprise. The
[Pisces Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-pisces/) here still reads rooms
and absorbs moods like the rest of the sign, but attachments, once
made, do not dissolve. People from every era of this person's life
remain in it. Loyalty operates less like a decision than like a
current: quiet, continuous, and stronger than it looks from shore.

## Devotion, at home and on the clock

Partners of a March 4 discover the archive early, anniversaries kept
without reminders, preferences remembered from a single mention. It
is a remarkable thing to be loved by someone who forgets nothing
about you. At work the same faculty becomes institutional memory: the
colleague who knows why the process exists, what was tried in 2019,
and which client hates surprises. Teams undervalue it right up until
this person is out for a week.

## Where the holding-on hurts

A memory that keeps everything keeps the expired things too, the
friendship over in all but name, the grievance old enough to walk,
the version of a person who stopped existing years ago. Nostalgia is
honest here, but it can crowd the present. The work is an occasional
inventory: keep what is still alive, thank the rest, and let the tide
have it.

A birthday pins exactly one thing in the sky, the Sun's place. The
rest of that day's sky kept moving by the minute, which is why the
[birth chart calculator](/birth-chart/) asks for your time and town
before it says anything at all.
