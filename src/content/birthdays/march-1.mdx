March opens a third of the way into [Pisces](/pisces/), past the
sign's dreamiest opening degrees and into its second decan, the
stretch the old triplicity system gives to the Moon. The assignment
reads like a character note. This is the cut of Pisces whose
imagination points at people: where early-sign fish drift toward
images and ideas, the March 1 kind keeps finding faces in the water.

## A caretaker's cut of Pisces

A [Pisces Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-pisces/) picks up feeling the
way other placements pick up facts, and the Moon decan turns that
reception into tending. People born today register the flat tone in a
short reply, the friend gone one shade quieter, the colleague running
on fumes, and respond before anyone asks. From outside it looks
selfless. From inside it is closer to reflex: other people's states
arrive like weather, and this Pisces would simply rather everyone be
dry and warm. What rarely gets announced, at any volume, is its own
forecast.

## In close ties and on the job

Partners get attention of unusual quality, someone genuinely curious
about their inner story, slow to judge, quick to comfort. What they
don't always get is warning when the giver runs low. At work this
wiring makes the person teams quietly rely on: the one who hears the
real problem underneath the stated one and onboards the new hire
without being asked. Fields where reading people is the actual job
(teaching, health, research with humans in it, hospitality) fit far
better than roles that treat all that reception as a distraction.

## The edge worth naming

The recurring snag is asymmetry. A March 1 Pisces can spend a season
absorbing everyone else's weather, call it fine, then grieve that
nobody checked on the barometer in return, having never mentioned
there was one. The fix is unglamorous: say the need out loud while it
is still small. The people who matter take direction better than
expected.

The date settles the Sun and nothing else. The Moon, loud in this
decan, moves into a new sign roughly every two and a half days, and
the rising sign turns with the hour, so the rest of the chart is a
question of clock and map. The
