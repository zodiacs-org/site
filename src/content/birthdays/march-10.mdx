March 10 sits on a border most horoscope columns never mention: the
line between the second and third decans of [Pisces](/pisces/).
Tradition puts the Moon on one side and Mars on the other, and the
Sun's exact degree on this date depends on your birth year and hour.
Some March 10s get the last of the caretaker's decan, others the
first of the fighter's.

## Two decans, one date

On the Moon side, the [Pisces Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-pisces/)
reads as the classic tender build: attuned, protective, moved by
people and quick to shelter them. On the Mars side the water picks up
iron: the same empathy, now with an engine behind it, quicker to act
on what it feels and noticeably harder to push around. Most people
born on this hinge describe both voices: a soft first response to the
world, and a firm second one that surprises anyone who mistook the
softness for the whole story.

## The blend in love and work

Partners get tenderness with follow-through: comfort through the
hard week, and an advocate when something must actually be said to
the landlord. Work benefits the same way: this date can hold a
distressed client and a deadline in the same afternoon. Fields that
need both reception and push (medicine, crisis work, producing, case
management) feel purpose-built for it.

## The edge on either side

Each decan ships its own friction. The Moon side over-absorbs and
under-announces; the Mars side swallows irritation until it surfaces
as a sudden cold front. The shared work is the same sentence spoken
earlier (the need, the no, the this-far) before the pressure does
the phrasing for you.

Which side of the line your Sun actually fell on is not a matter of
opinion: it's a degree, and it's computable. The
[birth chart calculator](/birth-chart/) takes your birth year, time,
and place, and names your decan along with everything else the sky
was doing that minute.
