The first decan of Aries gets its final full date on March 29, and
the decan goes out the way it came in: hungry for unbroken ground.
This is the pioneer's cut of [Aries](/aries/), Mars' own degrees,
happiest at whatever edge hasn't been settled yet.

## A pioneer's requirement

Most people enjoy some novelty; the March 29
[Aries Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-aries/) requires unmapped
territory the way fires require air. This is the colleague who
volunteers for the office nobody's opened, the region no one covers,
the problem with no literature; the friend whose vacations bend
toward places without guidebooks. Repetition doesn't bore this date
so much as suffocate it. Tellingly, a March 29 would usually rather
be first somewhere small than fifth somewhere grand.

## New ground in love and livelihood

In relationships, the pioneer pattern deserves honest handling: the
opening chapters (pursuit, discovery, the first apartment) are this
birthday's natural habitat, so a long partnership has to keep finding
genuine frontiers. The business built together, the country neither
has seen, the skill learned side by side all count. Partners who
mistake settledness for arrival will watch the horizon-hunger turn
elsewhere. At work the fit writes itself: founding teams, new
territories, field research, any first-person-in role. The tenth year
of maintaining anything is someone else's calling.

## The trail of unfinished starts

The friction is what pioneering leaves behind: a string of base camps
and no city. Half-built things accumulate wherever this date has
been. The reframe that helps is that depth is also a frontier.
Nobody has mapped the far side of a ten-year commitment either, and
almost no one gets there. Staying, past the point where novelty ends,
turns out to be unexplored country of its own.

The date lights the fuse and leaves. Where the charge is set (career, kin, craft, cause) is placed by the houses of your exact minute, and the [calculator](/birth-chart/) maps the blast pattern gently.
