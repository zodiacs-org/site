December 21 is the date the December solstice most often lands on,
and that makes it a genuine boundary birthday, whatever a magazine
sidebar may have told you. In roughly half the years since 1940 the
Sun spends this entire date finishing [Sagittarius](/sagittarius/);
in the rest it crosses into [Capricorn](/capricorn/) partway through
the day, and everyone born after that moment belongs to the Sea-Goat.
No year on record here gives Capricorn the whole day, yet plenty of
December 21 birthdays are Capricorns all the same. The table above
holds the year-by-year answer.

## Fire's last word, earth's first

The [Sagittarius Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-sagittarius/) ends its
sign wanting meaning; the
[Capricorn Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-capricorn/) opens its sign
wanting structure. A birthday on the hinge tends to carry both
briefs: the explorer's certainty that there is more out there, and
the builder's refusal to come home without something to show for the
trip. People born this date often describe an internal relay: the
big vision sketched at Sagittarius speed, then handed to an inner
foreman who checks the load-bearing walls.

## Adventure with a foundation

In love, the blend reads as warmth with follow-through: the partner
who proposes the wild trip and also checks the weather window. Work
is where the combination shines. Few birthdays are better built for
turning a vision into a standing thing: the book finished, the school
founded, the route not just traveled but mapped for the people
coming after.

## The throttle and the brake

The friction is internal. One voice says leap, the other says wait,
and both are persuasive, so under stress this date can oscillate,
reckless where caution was due and cautious where the leap was safe.
The growth edge is sequencing instead of arguing: let the fire choose
the destination and the earth choose the pace. They make better
colleagues than opponents.
