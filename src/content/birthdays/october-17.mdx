By mid-October the year is editing itself (trees down to structure,
daylight down to essentials), and late [Libra](/libra/) works the
same way. October 17 sits in the sign's Mercury decan with the arc
past its crest: less about charming the room now, more about getting
things exactly right.

## The editor's eye

Discernment leads. This date sees what to remove (from an essay, a
schedule, a crowded weekend), and its taste operates by subtraction
rather than accumulation. Compliments from an October 17 mean
something because inflation never touched them. A late-decan
[Libra Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-libra/) still has the sign's
diplomacy in full; it just carries a red pencil now, and uses it
first on its own drafts.

## Fewer, better bonds

The circle tightens with age, on purpose. Partners get a person who
chose them against real alternatives and re-chooses on the ordinary
mornings, which is more romantic than it sounds out loud. Work runs
toward refinement: review, editing, strategy, the second pass that
turns competent into finished. Give this birthday a bloated project
and a free hand, and watch a third of it disappear without anyone
missing what left.

## The second-guess habit

Revision applied to settled decisions is the trap: the accepted job
reopened at 2 a.m., the sent message reread for tone, the chosen
sofa relitigated in three stores. The growth is a statute of
limitations on your own verdicts. Decisions get one appeal, on new
evidence only, and then they stand. The editing gift is for drafts,
not for a life already in print.
