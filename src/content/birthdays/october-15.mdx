Ask an October 15 to summarize a position they oppose, and you'll
hear it argued better than its supporters manage. That's the Mercury
decan of [Libra](/libra/) doing what it does: fairness fluent enough
to voice any side of anything, a gift with an obvious upside and a
less obvious bill.

## The advocate's gift

Restatement is the talent. This date can put your position into words
you'd sign while privately disagreeing with every one of them, and
the effect on groups is measurable: meetings run shorter, apologies
land cleaner, and both parties leave feeling heard because both
parties literally were. A
[Libra Sun](/learn/placements/sun-in-libra/) sees the two sides by
default; the Mercury decan adds the rare ability to speak both
dialects without an accent.

## Words as a day job

In love, the flirtation is verbal and so is the repair. This
couple's real fights are about phrasing, which is lucky, because
phrasing is fixable by Tuesday. Partners get a person who actually
answers the question asked. Work belongs where a case must be made
well: advocacy, editing, diplomacy, the honest end of persuasion.
The title may lag; the reputation never does.

## Winning rooms, losing points

The friction: eloquence strong enough to prevail while mistaken, and
the queasy awareness of it afterward. Worse, the same rhetoric can
argue October 15 out of its own sound instincts. The growth is a
short private list of positions that are not up for debate, including
with yourself. Language is the tool here; it shouldn't get a vote.
