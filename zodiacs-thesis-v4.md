# Why Zodiacs Matter

Your sign already shows up in birthday wishes, dating profiles and group chats. Now it is also a coin you can own, give and prove is yours.

*Updated 13 Nov 2026 · Evidence checked 10 Nov 2026*

---

*Zodiacs.org runs Astrofolio and wrote this essay. We hold some of these coins and take no fee when you buy one. They are thinly traded and can fall to zero.* [Full disclosures](/disclosure/)

Before you had a username, you had a sign. Somebody told you when you were small, and you have known it ever since, whether or not you ever read a horoscope. YouGov once showed Americans the twelve signs and asked them to pick their own. [Nine in ten could](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/42292-one-four-americans-say-they-believe-astrology), although only about one in four said they believe in astrology. Very few ideas are known by nearly everyone and owned by no one, and this one has held that position for about 2,400 years.

[FIGURE fig-know. "Almost everyone knows their sign. Few say they believe." Two bars: know their sign 90%, say they believe in astrology 27%. YouGov, U.S. adults, April 2022.]

It got there by moving. The twelve signs took shape in Babylon and travelled on clay tablets, then in manuscripts, then in printed almanacs. In 1930 a London newspaper printed a horoscope for the newborn Princess Margaret, readers asked for more, and [the horoscope column was born](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/how-are-horoscopes-still-thing-180957701/). The phone put a sign in every pocket, and today the zodiac is some of the most dependable material on social media. Search any app for your sign and you will find posts from the last hour, most of them made by people who were paid nothing to make them.

[FIGURE fig-1. "The transmission." Clay tablet, star map, manuscript, almanac, newspaper (1930), phone, coin (5 Jul 2024). Caption: *The twelve-sign vocabulary survived by moving with people. Coins are its newest form.*]

In July 2024 twelve coins appeared on Solana, one for each sign. They are memecoins. Anyone can buy one, send one to a friend, and check on a public ledger that it is the one we list. We run the registry that lists them.

Most memecoins do not last a day. [CoinGecko studied](https://www.coingecko.com/research/publications/average-lifespan-of-pumpfun-tokens) 18.7 million coins launched on Pump.fun between January 2024 and June 2026 and found that 69% of them traded for the last time on the day they were created. Fewer than one in twenty were still trading ninety days later. A memecoin is built on a joke, and a joke is funniest the first time. The coin lasts as long as people keep retelling it, which for most of them is an afternoon.

[FIGURE fig-lifespan. "Most memecoins stop trading the day they are made." Bars: Pump.fun coins trading after their first day 31%, after their second day 20%, after ninety days under 5%; the twelve zodiac coins once the trade check is recorded. CoinGecko, updated 23 June 2026, counting trades on Pump.fun only.]

These twelve are in their third year, and we think the reason is their subject. A meme about a dog is about the dog. A meme about Scorpio is about you, and you will still be a Scorpio when the joke of the month has been forgotten. The zodiac also keeps its own calendar. Every month the Sun enters a new sign, everyone born under it has a birthday coming, and the group chat fills up with it again. Nobody has to schedule any of that.

[FIGURE fig-2. "People keep looking up the signs." Daily English Wikipedia reads across all twelve sign articles, August 2025 to July 2026, with each sign's season marked. This measures curiosity, not demand for the coins.]

You do with one what people already do with their sign. Your friend is a Leo, so on her birthday you send her Leo with a note only she would understand, and it arrives in her own wallet, where no app can take it back. A couple swaps, so that he holds her Cancer and she holds his Taurus, which is a small gesture in the way that wearing someone’s initial is a small gesture. Or you keep your own, as people have always worn their sign on a necklace. A picture of a lion can be copied forever, but a Leo that leaves your wallet has left it. And because our list of the twelve addresses is public, any app can recognise the listed Leo without asking us, so a dating profile could show it and a horoscope app could greet it.

Anyone can launch another Leo this afternoon, and the copy will have the name without the history. Each of these coins still has the Solana address it was [created with on 5 July 2024](/disclosure/#origin), and nobody can make more of them, including us. They have also lived through things a new coin has not. In [February 2025](https://x.com/stoolpresidente/status/1891528769770279114) Dave Portnoy bought our Libra by mistake while trying to buy a different coin of the same name, and he told his followers so in a post that has now been seen about three million times.

> Anybody wanna buy some fake Libra? Warning. This is a meme coin. I bought it by accident. It will be volatile. It will eventually go to zero.
>
> [Dave Portnoy · @stoolpresidente · 17 Feb 2025](https://x.com/stoolpresidente/status/1891528769770279114)

We kept that post on our record. We kept the critics too, including the ones who said a few wallets held too much and the ones who called the coins dead.

[FIGURE fig-provenance. "The public past includes the awkward parts." Four dated cards: the twelve are created, concentration is challenged, Portnoy buys by mistake, "dead bag" criticism.]

More than two years on, about 2,800 wallets hold Leo alone. [Zodiacs.org](/) is a free astrology site with birth charts, compatibility readings and daily horoscopes in five languages, and plugins that bring it into [ChatGPT and Claude](/apps/). People come for the astrology. The coins sit one labelled link away, for the ones who want to own their sign as well as read about it.

A memecoin can go to zero, and these can too. Portnoy said so in the post above. Trading in them is thin, and on a slow day the total traded in one of them is less than the price of a lunch. The ten largest accounts hold between 23% and 31% of each coin, a figure that includes the trading pool. No outside app has adopted them yet. We publish all of it, with the numbers, on [a scoreboard](#the-candidacy) below this essay, because we would rather you checked our claim than took it on trust.

The zodiac finds something new for people to do in every medium it reaches, and a public ledger lets them hold their sign. We expect people and apps to settle on a single set of twelve coins, as they settled long ago on a single set of twelve names, and we are making the case that it should be this set.
